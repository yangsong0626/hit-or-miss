Target brief

Why it matters

VEGF-A signals through VEGFR2 (KDR) on endothelial cells to drive angiogenesis and vascular leak. This feeds tumour blood supply and drives neovascular eye disease. Anti-VEGF agents such as bevacizumab and aflibercept have long been approved in cancer and in nAMD and DME, so the target is validated. Competition now centres on immunotherapy combinations, bispecifics and longer-acting delivery.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • PD-(L)1/VEGF bispecifics are the most common modality tracked here (23 programs) and have produced the clearest recent win: in first-line advanced biliary tract cancer, ivonescimab plus chemotherapy beat durvalumab plus chemotherapy on overall survival (HARMONi-GI1).
  • Adding a VEGFR TKI to PD-1 blockade does not work in every tumour. Lenvatinib plus pembrolizumab missed its ORR endpoint in cervical cancer (NCT05007106), and sitravatinib plus nivolumab missed overall survival in phase 3 (NCT03906071). Benefit looks indication-specific, not a class effect.
  • Business stops outnumber efficacy failures (6 vs 4). Lenvatinib in oesophageal cancer (NCT04949256), rivoceranib in adenoid cystic carcinoma (NCT04119453) and an axitinib combination stopped with no safety concerns (NCT05949632) reflect portfolio choices, not evidence against the mechanism.
  • In the eye the mechanism is settled. Recent readouts were mostly safety or descriptive studies (NCT06491914, NCT06190093). Competition has moved to reducing injection burden, through sustained-release TKIs such as EYP-1901 and OTX-TKI and 11 gene-therapy programs.

Where the openings are

  • Durability in nAMD: pending readouts for EYP-1901 (NCT06683742), tarcocimab tedromer (NCT06556368) and ABBV-RGX-314 (NCT04514653) should show whether sustained or one-time delivery can maintain vision with fewer injections, and which patients suit each approach.
  • Bispecific programs cluster in NSCLC and solid tumours broadly. The biliary result suggests GI cancers and other settings where PD-1 alone is weak may still be open. Biomarkers that predict who gains from adding VEGF blockade are missing, including in cervical cancer, where TKI-IO failed.
  • Rare tumours are underexplored: cabozantinib in osteosarcoma stopped for slow enrolment (NCT06341712), leaving the question unanswered. Pending readouts for fruquintinib in RCC (NCT05522231) and rivoceranib in HCC (NCT04639180) will show whether newer TKIs add anything beyond current standards.
