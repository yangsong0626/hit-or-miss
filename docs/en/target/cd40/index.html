Target brief

Why it matters

CD40 is a TNF-receptor family member on dendritic cells, B cells and macrophages. When CD40L on activated T cells binds it, antigen-presenting cells mature and prime CD8 T-cell responses. Agonizing CD40 could turn immunologically cold tumors hot, while blocking CD40–CD40L can damp excess inflammation and autoimmunity.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. All four programs tracked here are at Phase 2 or earlier, with no readouts and no failures or early stops. No trial reaches primary completion in the next six months, so nothing scheduled will test the mechanism soon.

Where the openings are

  • The programs tracked here are mostly in cancer (melanoma, soft tissue sarcoma, metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, solid tumors), plus one covering sepsis and community-acquired pneumonia. No program tracked here addresses CD40 antagonism in autoimmunity or transplant, which may be worth assessing.
  • Besides two antibodies, the field includes the small molecule TQB2916 and the peptide OPT101. An open question is whether non-antibody agents can cluster the receptor enough to agonize it, or give exposure and tolerability that differ from systemic antibodies.
  • No tracked program shows biomarker-based selection or delivery confined to the tumor. Selecting patients by myeloid infiltrate or dendritic-cell density, or activating CD40 only locally, could be ways to stand apart from the early antibodies.
