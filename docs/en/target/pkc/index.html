Target brief

Why it matters

PKC is a family of serine/threonine kinases. PKCβ sits downstream of BTK and PLCγ2 in B-cell receptor (BCR) signaling and drives the NF-κB activation that keeps CLL cells alive. PKCθ carries T-cell receptor signals. Blocking PKC below the BCR could reach tumors that resist drugs aimed at targets higher up the pathway.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. This site tracks one program: MingSight's small molecule MS-553, in Phase 1/2 for CLL/SLL. It has no readouts or failures, and no trials reach primary completion in the next six months, so the mechanism has not yet been tested.

Where the openings are

  • PKCβ acts downstream of BTK and PLCγ2. That makes it worth testing whether PKC inhibitors such as MS-553 still work in CLL with BTK or PLCG2 resistance mutations, and using those mutations to select patients.
  • Among programs tracked here, PKC has one small molecule from one company. No tracked program covers combinations with BCL2 inhibitors such as venetoclax, or other B-cell lymphomas that depend on BCR signaling.
  • PKCθ has a role in T-cell activation, yet no tracked program targets autoimmune or inflammatory disease. Isoform-selective inhibitors could be evaluated in these diseases.
