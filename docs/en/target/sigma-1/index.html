Target brief

Why it matters

Sigma-1 is a chaperone at the contact zone between the endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondria, where it regulates calcium signalling, ER stress and autophagy. Loss-of-function SIGMAR1 mutations cause juvenile ALS and distal hereditary motor neuropathy, which gives the target human genetic support. Agonists are being explored for neurodegenerative and psychiatric disease, and antagonists for pain.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. Only two small-molecule programs are tracked here, an Anavex Phase 2 program and Luye's LY03015 in Phase 1, and neither has a readout. No trial reaches primary completion in the next six months, so no near-term test of the target is scheduled.

Where the openings are

  • No tracked program addresses motor neuron disease linked to SIGMAR1 mutations. A proof-of-concept study in a genetically defined population could test more directly whether modulating the receptor changes disease course.
  • Only one tracked program lists schizophrenia, and every program is a small molecule. SIGMAR1 genotype or receptor-occupancy PET could be tested as tools for patient selection and dose finding, making efficacy signals easier to read.
  • No antagonist or pain program is tracked here. Sigma-1 antagonism has preclinical rationale in neuropathic pain, which remains white space among tracked programs.
