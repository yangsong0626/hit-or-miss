Target brief

Why it matters

TMPRSS6 (matriptase-2) cleaves hemojuvelin and so suppresses hepcidin. Hepcidin degrades ferroportin, which limits iron absorption and the release of stored iron. Mimicking hepcidin, silencing TMPRSS6 or blocking ferroportin cuts the iron available for red-cell production. In polycythemia vera this controls hematocrit without phlebotomy, and in hereditary hemochromatosis it corrects iron overload caused by hepcidin deficiency.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. The only formally tested registry readout is Rusfertide's Phase 3 VERIFY (NCT05210790), which met its primary endpoint in polycythemia vera. A curated result for divesiran (SANRECO) in the same disease was also positive. One is a peptide hepcidin mimetic and the other an siRNA, so both support the pathway, but together they cannot yet show where efficacy or safety limits lie. The next test is SLN124's Phase 1/2 NCT05499013, with primary completion listed for 2026-09 and results still pending.

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, four target polycythemia vera and only one targets hereditary hemochromatosis. Iron-overload diseases where hepcidin deficiency is the direct cause remain largely untested for this mechanism, a hypothesis worth pursuing for indication expansion.
  • Polycythemia vera is getting crowded, and dosing may be how programs stand apart. The siRNA and the ASO (e.g., sapablursen) allow long dosing intervals, the oral small molecule vamifeport is in Phase 2, and frequently injected peptides are the comparator. Dosing frequency, adherence and managing long-term iron deficiency are open comparisons.
  • Grouping patients by baseline iron indices or phlebotomy burden could show who benefits most. Efficacy and safety when combined with cytoreductive therapy are also not addressed by the readouts so far.
