Target brief

Why it matters

ALK fusions such as EML4-ALK keep the kinase switched on and drive roughly 3–5% of non-small cell lung cancer. ALK alterations also drive anaplastic large-cell lymphoma and some neuroblastoma, where they are mostly point mutations. Crizotinib, alectinib and lorlatinib are approved, but on-target resistance mutations and brain metastases still limit long-term control.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The tracked clinical record is still thin. The only readout, NCT03194893, is a descriptive alectinib safety study with no efficacy test. The first real tests will be the alectinib Phase 3 trials NCT06624059 and NCT05170204 and the Phase 2/3 ALK-positive NSCLC trial NCT05987956.

Where the openings are

  • Ten of the 12 tracked programs are small molecules. Neladalkib is in Phase 3, while TRI-611, SIM0518 and anecatibin are early. A testable hypothesis: these drugs can stand out by covering compound mutations such as G1202R, working in the brain and sparing TRK, rather than as first-line copies.
  • The Ashibio antibody and Diagonal's DIAG723 are the only antibody programs. ALK fusion proteins have no extracellular domain, so antibodies more plausibly work on full-length ALK receptor, as in neuroblastoma. The one tracked cardiovascular/metabolic trial also suggests a metabolic angle worth exploring.
  • Tracked programs focus mostly on NSCLC, and only one targets ALK fusion-positive solid or CNS tumors. Open gaps: tumor-agnostic enrollment covering anaplastic large-cell lymphoma, inflammatory myofibroblastic tumor and pediatric tumors, and ctDNA-guided grouping of patients by resistance mutation.
