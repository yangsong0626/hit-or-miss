Target brief

Why it matters

VWF is a multimeric plasma glycoprotein that tethers platelets to subendothelial collagen through GPIb and carries FVIII, slowing its clearance. VWF defects cause von Willebrand disease (VWD), the most common inherited bleeding disorder; type 1 is a partial quantitative deficiency. Conversely, excess VWF activity drives microvascular thrombosis in TTP.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. Only one program is tracked here, a phase 1/2 study by Hemab ApS in VWD including type 1. It has no readouts, and no trials reach primary completion in the next six months, so there is no near-term test of the mechanism.

Where the openings are

  • Programs tracked here address only type 1 VWD. Types 2 and 3, where desmopressin helps little, remain open. An unanswered question is whether therapies acting on endogenous VWF can work when it is nearly absent.
  • Type 1 VWD is heterogeneous: some patients mainly clear VWF faster (high VWFpp/VWF:Ag ratio), others mainly make less. Stratifying by such markers could show which mechanism fits which patients. Women with heavy menstrual bleeding are a practical endpoint population.
  • No VWF-inhibiting programs are tracked here, though anti-VWF blockade is clinically validated in acquired TTP (caplacizumab). Arterial thrombosis such as ischemic stroke, where VWF–GPIb binding under high shear matters, is a hypothesis worth testing, with bleeding risk assessed alongside.
