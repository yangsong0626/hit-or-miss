Target brief

Why it matters

RET is a receptor tyrosine kinase that signals through RAS–MAPK. Fusions such as KIF5B-RET drive about 1–2% of non-small cell lung cancers and some papillary thyroid cancers. Activating mutations such as M918T drive medullary thyroid cancer, and germline mutations cause MEN2. The approval of the selective inhibitor selpercatinib confirmed RET as an oncogenic driver.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record here is still thin. All four RET programs tracked here are small molecules, and none has a readout or failure on record. No trial reaches primary completion in the next six months, so no near-term test is scheduled. Eli Lilly's selpercatinib (phase 3) leads; Hansoh, Ellipses (EP0031) and Taiho are in phase 1/2–2.

Where the openings are

  • The Hansoh, EP0031 and Taiho programs trail selpercatinib. One testable hypothesis is that keeping activity against resistance mutations acquired on first-generation selective inhibitors, such as solvent-front G810 changes, is the clearest way to stand apart.
  • Every program tracked here is a small molecule aimed at lung or thyroid cancer. Degraders, combinations that target MET- or KRAS-driven bypass resistance, and RET-fusion tumors outside the lung and thyroid are all untested among tracked programs.
  • Infantile myofibromatosis appears among the listed conditions, which points to work in rare pediatric tumors. It remains open whether systematic RET screening in pediatric and tumor-agnostic settings can find enough patients.
