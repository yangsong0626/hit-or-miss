Target brief

Why it matters

GABA-A receptors are pentameric chloride channels that carry most fast inhibitory signalling in the brain. Neurosteroids positively modulate both synaptic receptors and extrasynaptic δ-containing receptors, boosting tonic inhibition. Too little inhibition is linked to epileptic encephalopathies and perinatal mood disorders, and benzodiazepines long ago showed the target can be drugged.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record tracked here is still thin. Both programs are Phase 3 small molecules: Immedica's ganaxolone and an unnamed Lipocine compound. Neither has a readout, failure or early stop on record, and no trial reaches primary completion in the next six months, so no near-term test is scheduled.

Where the openings are

  • Tracked programs cover only CDKL5 deficiency disorder and postpartum depression. Other developmental and epileptic encephalopathies, and depression outside the postpartum setting, are hypotheses for testing whether the neurosteroid mechanism carries over.
  • Both programs are small-molecule positive modulators. No subunit-selective agents, such as α2/α3- or δ-extrasynaptic-selective molecules, are tracked here. Such agents might separate efficacy from sedation, which makes them a differentiation idea worth testing.
  • The Lipocine program, registered in September 2026, offers a chance to see whether route and treatment length set a postpartum depression therapy apart. Pharmacodynamic markers such as EEG beta power could guide dosing and patient selection.
