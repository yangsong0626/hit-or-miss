Target brief

Why it matters

GBA1 encodes the lysosomal enzyme GCase. Biallelic mutations cause Gaucher disease, and heterozygous variants are the most common genetic risk factor for Parkinson's disease. Low GCase activity lets glucosylceramide build up and promotes alpha-synuclein aggregation. Restoring enzyme activity with chaperones, activators or gene therapy is therefore pursued as a disease-modifying strategy.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. The only readout, ambroxol in NCT06193421, was stopped early by sponsor decision. That is a business reason and is not evidence against the mechanism. No tracked trial reaches primary completion in the next six months.

Where the openings are

  • Enrolling patients by GBA1 variant severity, or selecting them with biomarkers such as GCase activity or glucosylsphingosine, may show signals earlier. Few programs tracked here explicitly limit enrollment to a GBA1 population.
  • All three programs are in phase 1/2: two small molecules and one Capsida gene therapy. Among programs tracked here, none uses brain-penetrant enzyme replacement or other modalities. Which delivery route best restores brain GCase is still an open question.
  • All tracked trials are in Parkinson's disease. GBA1 variants are also a strong risk factor for dementia with Lewy bodies. That indication, and sporadic PD with low GCase activity, are not addressed by any program tracked here.
