Target brief

Why it matters

CNP signals through NPR-B (NPR2) on growth-plate chondrocytes. It raises cGMP and damps the MAPK signaling that gain-of-function FGFR3 drives in achondroplasia. The genetics are clear: NPR2 loss-of-function causes severe short stature, and CNP excess causes overgrowth. Vosoritide, a daily CNP analog, is approved for achondroplasia.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The record is thin. Weekly navepegritide met its growth-velocity endpoint in achondroplasia (NCT05598320). Vosoritide's Noonan trial (NCT06668805) was stopped, citing feasibility and the treatment landscape, so it says little about the mechanism. No tracked trial reads out in the next six months.

Where the openings are

  • Indications beyond achondroplasia remain open. Hypochondroplasia (2 programs) and idiopathic short stature (1) are tracked, and Noonan was dropped. Noonan's RAS/MAPK overactivity makes CNP biologically plausible there, so it may be worth revisiting.
  • In idiopathic short stature, heterozygous NPR2 loss-of-function variants or growth-plate biomarkers (e.g., the collagen X marker CXM) could be tested as a way to select likely responders by genotype.
  • All four tracked programs are injected peptides from two companies. Oral or small-molecule NPR-B agonists, longer dosing intervals, combinations with FGFR3-directed agents, and endpoints beyond height (proportionality, foramen magnum stenosis) have no data here.
