Target brief

Why it matters

Class I PI3K isoforms convert PIP2 to PIP3 and activate AKT–mTOR signaling, which controls cell growth, survival and metabolism. PIK3CA mutations and PTEN loss are common in endometrial and prostate cancer, and PTEN-null tumors often rely on PI3Kβ. Platelet PI3Kβ also drives thrombus formation under high shear, which extends the target to stroke.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. All 8 programs tracked here are in Phase 1–2, none has a readout or failure on record, and no trials reach primary completion in the next six months, so no near-term data point will test the mechanism.

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, most target endometrial cancer (3) and mCRPC (2), two tumor types where PTEN loss is common. Enrolling by PTEN or PIK3CA status could test whether isoform selectivity leads to different efficacy.
  • TBO-309 (ischemic stroke and tandem occlusion) is the only non-oncology program tracked here. An open question is whether PI3Kβ antiplatelet activity can improve reperfusion without adding bleeding risk.
  • Every tracked program with a known modality is a small molecule. Pan-PI3K inhibition is often limited by insulin-feedback hyperglycemia, so isoform- or mutant-selective agents, new dosing schedules, or degraders are possible ways to differentiate.
