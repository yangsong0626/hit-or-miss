Target brief

Why it matters

SGLT2, encoded by SLC5A2, handles most glucose reabsorption in the proximal tubule. Blocking it causes glycosuria and natriuresis and lowers intraglomerular pressure through tubuloglomerular feedback. Familial renal glycosuria from SLC5A2 loss-of-function is largely benign, which supports the target genetically. Empagliflozin and dapagliflozin have long been approved, and their use has grown from type 2 diabetes to heart failure and CKD.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record tracked here is still thin. In most readouts so far the SGLT2 inhibitor was a combination backbone, not the drug being tested: LEGEND (NCT05232071, met) and CONFIDENCE (NCT05254002, no formal test). The first real tests will be NCT06481891 (sotagliflozin, HCM), NCT07107945 and NCT06926660 (empagliflozin, CKD), and NCT07093476 (type 2 diabetes).

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, only sotagliflozin is being studied in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (NCT06481891, obstructive), and a non-obstructive indication is also listed. These trials can test whether dual SGLT1/2 inhibition helps the heart muscle in structural cardiomyopathy, and whether obstructive and non-obstructive patients respond differently.
  • Combinations are the main path forward. CONFIDENCE (NCT05254002) paired empagliflozin with a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist, and CSPC has announced positive topline results for a DPP-4 combination. Stratifying patients by baseline UACR or similar markers could show whether the kidney benefits add up.
  • Oncology has a single metastatic breast cancer/TNBC program. Untested hypotheses include SGLT2 inhibition offsetting the high blood sugar caused by PI3K-pathway drugs, or limiting how much glucose tumors take up.
