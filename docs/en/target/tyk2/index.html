Target brief

Why it matters

TYK2 is a JAK-family kinase that carries IL-23, IL-12 and type I interferon signals, central to psoriasis and lupus. The loss-of-function variant P1104A protects against several autoimmune diseases with only mild immune deficiency, which validates the target genetically. Allosteric inhibitors that bind the JH2 pseudokinase domain spare JAK1-3. Deucravacitinib is approved for plaque psoriasis.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The record is still thin: no registry readouts are tracked here. News reports positive psoriasis signals and a midstage Alumis lupus miss (no NCT). The first real tests are deucravacitinib's Phase 3 SLE trials NCT05620407 and NCT05617677 and its Sjögren's trial NCT05946941.

Where the openings are

  • Five of the six programs tracked here target plaque psoriasis, while Crohn's disease, hidradenitis suppurativa and vitiligo have one program each. These indications could test whether selective TYK2 inhibition stands out in IL-23- or interferon-driven disease beyond psoriasis.
  • Lupus and Sjögren's trials could enrich for patients with a type I interferon signature. The deucravacitinib SLE Phase 3 trials (NCT05620407, NCT05617677) should help show whether the Alumis miss reflects the class or that one molecule.
  • Every program tracked here is an oral small molecule. Degraders or topical formulations remain open, either for localized disease such as vitiligo or to test deeper target suppression.
