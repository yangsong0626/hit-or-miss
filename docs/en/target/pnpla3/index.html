Target brief

Why it matters

PNPLA3 encodes a protein that sits on lipid droplets in liver cells. Its common I148M variant is the strongest inherited risk factor for fatty liver disease, MASH, fibrosis and liver cancer. The mutant protein builds up on lipid droplets and blocks fat mobilisation, so lowering liver PNPLA3 in carriers is a genetically grounded way to treat MASH.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is thin. Among programs tracked here, the only readout is AstraZeneca's phase 2 FORTUNA trial of AZD2693 (NCT05809934). It missed its 52-week primary endpoint of NASH resolution without fibrosis worsening. No PNPLA3 trials reach primary completion in the next six months.

Where the openings are

  • It is unclear whether the FORTUNA miss (NCT05809934) reflects the mechanism or the endpoint. Analyses split by I148M dose (two copies vs one), liver-fat change and fibrosis markers could test whether benefit is limited to a narrower genetic group.
  • Among programs tracked here, AZD2693 is the only PNPLA3 program, and only one company is involved. Other approaches, such as siRNA or small molecules that selectively clear the 148M protein from lipid droplets, remain untested.
  • A 52-week histology endpoint may be too short or too insensitive for a genetic driver of slowly progressing fibrosis. Longer treatment, or pairing PNPLA3 lowering with systemic metabolic therapies, are hypotheses no one has tested yet.
