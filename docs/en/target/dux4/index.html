Target brief

Why it matters

DUX4 is an embryonic transcription factor that should be silent in adult muscle. In FSHD, D4Z4 repeat contraction at 4q35 (FSHD1) or SMCHD1 mutations (FSHD2) switch it back on when a permissive 4qA allele is present. It then activates downstream genes that kill muscle cells. This toxic gain of function gives knocking down DUX4 directly a clear genetic rationale.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. The site tracks one DUX4 program: an unnamed Sarepta siRNA in Phase 1/2. It has no readouts yet, and no trial reaches primary completion in the next six months, so no near-term readout will test the mechanism.

Where the openings are

  • siRNA is the only modality among programs tracked here. ASOs, antibody-oligonucleotide conjugates and epigenetic silencing of D4Z4 are untried here, and each would test a different way of reaching skeletal muscle.
  • DUX4 is expressed sporadically and at low levels, which makes it hard to measure directly. Using a signature of DUX4 target genes plus muscle MRI as pharmacodynamic and enrollment biomarkers may decide whether early trials can detect a signal.
  • With only one early program, the key questions are open: how much DUX4 knockdown is needed for a strength or function benefit, and whether FSHD1 and FSHD2 patients respond differently.
