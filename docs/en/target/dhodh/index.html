Target brief

Why it matters

DHODH sits in the inner mitochondrial membrane, catalyses the fourth step of de novo pyrimidine synthesis and is coupled to the electron transport chain. Fast-growing tumour cells lean heavily on de novo pyrimidine supply, which is the case for using it in oncology. Leflunomide and teriflunomide, long used in autoimmune disease, show the target can be drugged.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. The site tracks one Phase 1 small-molecule program in solid tumours, from Jabez. It has no readouts and no failures, and no trial reaches primary completion in the next six months, so no real clinical test is in sight.

Where the openings are

  • The tracked program covers solid tumours only. In blood cancers such as AML, DHODH inhibition can push cells to differentiate, and no program tracked here addresses that, which makes it a hypothesis worth testing.
  • Tumours can get around DHODH blockade through uridine salvage. Selecting patients by salvage capacity (for example UCK2 or nucleoside transporter expression), or adding a drug that blocks salvage, could improve single-agent activity.
  • Pyrimidine depletion may affect antigen presentation and metabolic stress. That gives a rationale for pairing DHODH inhibitors with immunotherapy or DNA-damaging agents, but no clinical data tracked here tests it yet.
