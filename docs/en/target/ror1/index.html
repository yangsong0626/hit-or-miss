Target brief

Why it matters

ROR1, a Wnt5a receptor, is an oncofetal antigen: high in embryos, low in most adult tissue, but re-expressed in CLL, mantle cell lymphoma and solid tumours such as triple-negative breast, lung adenocarcinoma and ovarian cancer. It supports tumour-cell survival and migration, and its tumour-restricted expression suits ADCs, bispecifics and CAR-T.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. Both programs tracked here, CTTQ's ADC TQB2101 and an unnamed NEOK Bio bispecific, are in phase 1. Neither has readouts, failures or early stops, and no trial reaches primary completion in the next six months. Their first phase 1 data will be the first real test.

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, one targets advanced haematologic cancers and one advanced solid tumours, and there is no CAR-T or other cell therapy. Cell therapy could be tested in ROR1-high CLL or mantle cell lymphoma.
  • A hypothesis to test: does ROR1 expression on IHC predict response to ADCs or bispecifics? Enrolling by expression level in triple-negative breast, lung adenocarcinoma or ovarian cancer could set a program apart from unselected basket designs.
  • ADCs and bispecifics work differently. Comparing their activity when target expression is low, and their toxicity in normal tissues with low ROR1, is the key question for early data.
