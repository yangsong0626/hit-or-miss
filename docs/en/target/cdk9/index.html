Target brief

Why it matters

CDK9 is the catalytic subunit of P-TEFb. It phosphorylates RNA polymerase II so that transcription can proceed past the start of a gene. Short-lived oncogenic transcripts such as MCL1 and MYC depend heavily on it, so cancers addicted to transcription, including blood cancers, may be vulnerable. Toxicity held back earlier pan-CDK inhibitors, and selective CDK9 inhibitors aim for a wider therapeutic window.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. This site tracks two phase 1/2 programs, GFH009 (Genfleet) and SLS009 (Sellas), both in large B-cell lymphoma and hematologic malignancies. Neither has a judged readout, and no tracked trial reaches primary completion in the next six months, so no near-term test of the mechanism is in view.

Where the openings are

  • The tracked programs cover only blood cancers. Solid tumors with MYC amplification or MCL1 dependence have no selective CDK9 program here, which leaves an indication hypothesis to test.
  • Only one tracked program is clearly a small molecule. CDK9 degraders and other new modalities do not appear yet. They could be tested for whether they blunt the transcriptional rebound after inhibition and are better tolerated on intermittent dosing.
  • Two ways an early program could set itself apart: selecting patients with MCL1/MYC dependency biomarkers, or combining with a BCL2 inhibitor such as venetoclax to block both anti-apoptotic routes.
