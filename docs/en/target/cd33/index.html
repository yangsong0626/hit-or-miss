Target brief

Why it matters

CD33 (Siglec-3) is a myeloid surface receptor found on leukemic blasts in most patients with AML, making it one of the best-validated antigens in the disease; the antibody-drug conjugate gemtuzumab ozogamicin is approved for CD33-positive AML. Because normal myeloid progenitors also express CD33, myelosuppression is the main limit for more potent modalities such as CAR cells.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • Among programs tracked here, CD33 has only two phase 1 programs (iCell Gene's anti-CD33-CLL1 CAR-T and Senti's SENTI-202), with no readouts or failures and no primary completions expected in the next six months, so the clinical record is still too thin to draw lessons.

Where the openings are

  • iCell Gene's CAR-T pairs CD33 with CLL1. Whether dual-antigen designs cover heterogeneous AML better without causing prolonged marrow aplasia is the core open question; no tracked readout answers it yet.
  • Among programs tracked here there are no CD33 ADCs, T-cell engagers or radioconjugates. Pairing potent CD33 agents with CD33-edited donor stem cells, so the graft is spared, is a hypothesis that could make post-transplant maintenance feasible.
  • Patient selection is largely unexplored: CD33 expression density and the rs12459419 splice variant, which removes the IgV-domain epitope, could predict who benefits. MDS appears in only one tracked program's indications.
