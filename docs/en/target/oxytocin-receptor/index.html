Target brief

Why it matters

The oxytocin receptor is a Gq-coupled GPCR that drives uterine contraction and, in the hypothalamus, helps regulate satiety and social behavior. People with Prader-Willi syndrome have fewer hypothalamic oxytocin neurons, so activating this receptor is a plausible way to reduce hyperphagia. Carbetocin, a long-acting oxytocin analog, has been used for postpartum hemorrhage in many countries for years.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. Carbetocin, the only program tracked here, missed its primary efficacy endpoint in the parent PWS hyperphagia study ACP-101-302. Its extension study NCT06420297 was then stopped early for efficacy reasons. No readouts are due in the next six months to serve as the next test.

Where the openings are

  • NCT06420297 stopped for efficacy, not safety. Whether a responsive PWS subgroup exists (by genetic subtype, age or baseline hyperphagia severity) is still open, and biomarker-stratified studies could test it.
  • The only program tracked here is an intranasal peptide. No small-molecule agonists or positive allosteric modulators with better brain penetration appear in the clinic yet. These could test whether peripheral dosing simply failed to engage the hypothalamus enough.
  • Among programs tracked here, nothing targets other oxytocin-linked indications such as social deficits in autism or obesity. There are no data yet on whether the PWS miss applies to those populations.
