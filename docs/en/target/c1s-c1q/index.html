Target brief

Why it matters

C1q binds antibody–antigen complexes and activates C1r and C1s, the proteases that start the classical complement pathway by cleaving C4 and C2. Blocking this step stops autoantibody-driven complement attack before C3 activation while largely sparing the lectin and alternative pathways, giving a rationale in antibody-mediated diseases such as CIDP and autoimmune hemolytic anemia.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The record is still thin: the only readout tracked is NCT04691570, Annexon's 7-patient descriptive phase 2 in wAIHA on safety and hemoglobin, neither a hit nor a miss. Sanofi's riliprubart phase 3 CIDP program has no readouts here, and nothing reaches primary completion in the next six months.

Where the openings are

  • In CIDP, riliprubart is the only classical-pathway program tracked here. Whether upstream C1s blockade stands apart from other mechanisms may depend on patient selection, such as autoantibody subclass or evidence of complement deposition; prospectively testing such markers could define who benefits.
  • wAIHA is mainly IgG-driven and complement involvement varies between patients. Beyond the small descriptive study NCT04691570, a controlled trial enriched for complement-activated patients (e.g., C3d-positive direct antiglobulin test) would test whether C1q/C1s blockade helps a defined subset.
  • Both programs tracked here are monoclonal antibodies, limited to CIDP and wAIHA. Other complement-fixing antibody diseases, such as multifocal motor neuropathy or antibody-mediated transplant rejection, and non-antibody modalities such as oral small molecules or siRNA remain untested hypotheses.
