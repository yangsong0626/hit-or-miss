Target brief

Why it matters

FAAH and MAGL break down the endocannabinoids anandamide and 2-AG. Blocking them raises endocannabinoid tone and activates CB1/CB2 indirectly, which could give pain-relieving, antispastic and anxiolytic effects without the psychoactivity of direct agonists. MAGL also supplies arachidonic acid for prostaglandin synthesis in the brain, which adds a rationale against neuroinflammation.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. Both programs tracked here are Bristol Myers Squibb Phase 2 assets, in MS spasticity and Alzheimer's agitation. None has read out, and no trials reach primary completion in the next six months, so no near-term readout will test the mechanism.

Where the openings are

  • The pathway tag covers pain, epilepsy and cough, yet the programs tracked here address only MS spasticity and Alzheimer's agitation. Neuropathic pain, refractory epilepsy and chronic cough remain untested hypotheses.
  • It is still unknown whether FAAH-only, MAGL-only or dual inhibition drives benefit, and which causes CB1-linked tolerance or CNS side effects. Plasma anandamide and 2-AG could serve as target-engagement markers to guide dosing and patient selection.
  • Among programs tracked here, only one company is active. Peripherally restricted MAGL inhibitors for inflammatory pain, or designs built around long-term tolerability, could offer other developers a way in.
