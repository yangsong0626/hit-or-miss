Target brief

Why it matters

EpCAM is an epithelial adhesion glycoprotein that is highly expressed on most adenocarcinomas and some cancer stem-like cells, which makes it a classic solid-tumour surface antigen. It is also expressed on normal epithelia, and that limits the therapeutic window. The EpCAM×CD3 bispecific catumaxomab was once approved in the EU for intraperitoneal treatment of malignant ascites, showing the target can deliver when given locally.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. This site tracks 7 programs from 7 companies, with no readouts, no failures and no trials reaching primary completion in the next six months. Wuhan YZY's Phase 3 bispecific is the most advanced, and its eventual readout will be the first real test.

Where the openings are

  • The central problem is still on-target, off-tumour toxicity from EpCAM on normal epithelia. A priority question is whether conditionally active or masked T-cell engagers (e.g., BA3182), or local intraperitoneal dosing, can widen the window.
  • The programs tracked here are mostly bispecifics and monoclonal antibodies. No ADC or radioligand appears yet, and cell and gene therapy have one program each, so these modalities are largely open.
  • Most programs enrol broad advanced solid tumours. Selecting patients by EpCAM expression or by tumour type (e.g., gastric or ovarian cancer with peritoneal spread) might give clearer early signals.
