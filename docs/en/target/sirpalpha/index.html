Target brief

Why it matters

SIRPα sits on macrophages, dendritic cells and other myeloid cells. When it binds CD47 on tumor cells it signals through SHP-1/2 to block phagocytosis, the "don't eat me" signal. CD47 is abundant on red cells, so hitting the receptor side instead may release phagocytosis with less anemia and other blood toxicity than anti-CD47 agents.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • All 6 programs tracked here, from 4 companies, are in Phase 1. None has a readout, failure or early stop on record, and no trials reach primary completion in the next six months. So the clinical case for the mechanism is untested, and it is unclear when the first real test will come.

Where the openings are

  • Most tracked programs are in advanced solid tumors, with only a few in lymphoma and myeloma. One hypothesis to test: whether boosting phagocytosis shows up more readily in blood cancers when paired with opsonizing antibodies such as rituximab.
  • The tracked programs use only two formats, monoclonal antibodies and SIRPα-Fc fusions. No bispecifics or ADCs appear. A tumor-targeted bispecific might confine myeloid activation to the tumor.
  • SIRPα comes in variants (V1/V2), and the related SIRPγ is found on T cells. How fully an antibody covers these variants, and whether it spares SIRPγ, could set programs apart and guide patient selection.
