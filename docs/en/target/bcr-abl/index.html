Target brief

Why it matters

BCR-ABL is the fusion kinase created by the t(9;22) translocation (the Philadelphia chromosome). It is constantly switched on and drives chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) and part of acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL). Imatinib made it the textbook validated target for targeted therapy. Newer work focuses on resistance mutations such as T315I, on tolerability, and on treatment-free remission.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. Only one readout exists: Asciminib's phase 3 ASC4START (NCT05456191) met its primary endpoint, time to stopping treatment because of adverse events. That shows tolerability can carry a frontline CML registration. The next test is Olverembatinib's phase 3 in GIST, NCT06640361.

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, frontline and resistant CML already has several phase 3 agents. To stand out, the phase 1 molecules from Enliven, CTTQ (TQB3911) and Hansoh may need evidence on T315I or compound mutations, long-term tolerability, or treatment-free remission rates.
  • Few tracked programs address newly diagnosed Ph+ ALL. TKI-based regimens with less chemotherapy, with treatment adjusted by minimal residual disease (MRD), remain a possible opening.
  • NCT06640361 will test whether Olverembatinib extends to GIST. Its activity in GIST likely comes from KIT/PDGFRA inhibition, not BCR-ABL, so selecting patients by mutation type may decide the result.
