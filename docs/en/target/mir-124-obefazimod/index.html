Target brief

Why it matters

Obefazimod (ABX464) is an oral small molecule that binds the cap-binding complex. This boosts splicing of a long non-coding RNA and raises miR-124, a microRNA that restrains inflammatory signaling such as IL-6/STAT3 and Th17 responses. The aim is to calm gut inflammation in ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease without broad immunosuppression, as an oral alternative to injectable biologics.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. The site logs both ABX464 Phase 3 ulcerative colitis trials (NCT05507216, NCT05507203) as having no formal hypothesis test. The ABX196 liver cancer study (NCT03897543) is a descriptive safety study and likely works by a different mechanism. No readouts are expected in the next six months.

Where the openings are

  • Crohn's disease is the clearest open question. Among programs tracked here, only one Phase 2 obefazimod study addresses it, and it has no readout yet. It is still untested whether a miR-124 mechanism aimed at mucosal inflammation carries over to transmural disease.
  • Tracked trials do not use patient selection. Baseline or on-treatment miR-124 levels in blood or colon tissue could be tested as a predictive or pharmacodynamic biomarker. That could help identify responders in a field crowded with oral and injectable options.
  • All tracked programs are the same molecule from one company. Among programs tracked here, no one has yet tried other miR-124 inducers or tested obefazimod combined with existing advanced therapies.
