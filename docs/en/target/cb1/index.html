Target brief

Why it matters

CB1 is among the most abundant GPCRs in the brain. On presynaptic terminals it mediates how endocannabinoids and THC dampen neurotransmitter release, shaping pain, appetite and mood. Peripheral CB1 also regulates liver and fat metabolism. Rimonabant, an inverse agonist once approved in the EU for obesity and later withdrawn for psychiatric adverse effects, confirmed the metabolic biology but exposed the central safety risk.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record tracked here is still thin. There is one Phase 2 small molecule from Sinfatin S.L, with two trials in chronic stroke and post-COVID-19 syndrome first posted in November 2026. There are no readouts yet, and no trial reaches primary completion in the next six months.

Where the openings are

  • None of the programs tracked here target obesity, metabolic liver disease or diabetic kidney disease, where the biology of peripheral CB1 is strongest. Peripherally restricted inverse agonists that stay out of the brain are an open hypothesis to test.
  • For the Sinfatin trials, the key unknowns are whether the compound activates or blocks CB1 and how much of it reaches the brain. Adding psychiatric safety monitoring and target-engagement markers would make the results easier to interpret.
  • Only small molecules appear here. Biased or allosteric modulators, and CB1 antibodies that do not cross the blood-brain barrier, could test whether peripheral benefit can be separated from central effects.
