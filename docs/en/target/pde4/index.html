Target brief

Why it matters

PDE4 is the main enzyme that breaks down cAMP in immune and airway cells. Blocking it dampens TNF, IL-17, IL-23 and other inflammatory signals, and it also has anti-fibrotic effects. Roflumilast (COPD) and apremilast (psoriasis, Behçet's and others) validated the mechanism, but nausea, diarrhoea and weight loss cap oral dosing. That limit drives work on subtype-selective, inhaled and topical agents.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The record here is thin. The only tracked readout, roflumilast in psoriasis (NCT05763082), had no formal statistical test, and no failures are logged. The first real tests will be nerandomilast in IPF and ILD (NCT06241560, NCT06238622, NCT06806592) and ensifentrine in COPD (NCT07016412).

Where the openings are

  • Fibrotic lung disease is the main new direction among programs tracked here. The nerandomilast phase 3 trials in IPF and broader ILD (NCT06238622, NCT06806592) should show whether PDE4B-preferential inhibition works across fibrotic phenotypes. Selecting patients by markers such as progression rate is still open.
  • Routes that limit systemic exposure offer a way to stand apart from oral drugs. Whether inhaled dual PDE3/4 inhibitor ensifentrine (NCT07016412) can extend beyond COPD into asthma or bronchiectasis is still unanswered.
  • Oral psoriasis is getting crowded. A press release reports prifemilast beat apremilast head-to-head in phase 3, and apremilast has another phase 3 pending (NCT06088199). New entrants likely need a clear tolerability or head-to-head edge. Systemic sclerosis-related ILD and Behçet's look less contested.
