Target brief

Why it matters

Aurora A (AURKA) is a mitotic kinase that controls centrosome maturation and spindle assembly, and it is amplified or overexpressed in many solid tumors. It binds and stabilizes MYCN, which links it to neuroblastoma. Preclinical work suggests RB1-deficient tumors depend on Aurora A, a synthetic-lethal relationship that offers a basis for biomarker-guided use.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. Only two small-molecule programs are tracked here: Jacobio's JAB-2485 (Phase 1/2) and an unnamed Eli Lilly molecule (Phase 1). Neither has a readout or a recorded failure, and no tracked trial reaches primary completion in the next six months, so no near-term test is in view.

Where the openings are

  • Patient selection may be the main opening. Prospective enrichment for RB1 loss, MYCN amplification or AURKA amplification would test whether the synthetic-lethal hypothesis turns into clinical benefit.
  • Every program tracked here is a kinase inhibitor. Degraders, or molecules that block the Aurora A–MYCN interaction, would also act on its non-catalytic scaffolding role, and none are tracked yet.
  • ER+ breast cancer is among the tracked indications. Whether combinations work after resistance to endocrine therapy or CDK4/6 inhibitors is still an open question.
