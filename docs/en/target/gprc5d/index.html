Target brief

Why it matters

GPRC5D is an orphan G protein-coupled receptor that is highly expressed on malignant plasma cells. In normal tissue it is largely confined to hair follicles, nail beds and tongue papillae, which makes it a workable myeloma target. Its expression does not depend on BCMA, so it offers a second route after BCMA therapy fails. The keratinised-tissue expression also predicts on-target skin, nail and taste toxicity.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clearest positive evidence comes from hitting GPRC5D and BCMA together. MonumenTAL-6 (talquetamab plus teclistamab) reported positive Phase 3 topline results, and IASO's BCMA/GPRC5D dual CAR-T RD140 showed a positive Phase 1 signal. Covering both antigens looks like the main direction.
  • Roche's forimtamig plus carfilzomib study (NCT06055075) was stopped early because the sponsor dropped forimtamig. It had a safety endpoint and no formal test, so the stop is not evidence against the mechanism. More likely it shows how hard it is for followers to stand apart from talquetamab.
  • Cell therapies make up 6 of the 12 programs tracked here, mostly Phase 1 studies from Chinese companies. None has yet produced a readout with a formal statistical test. The pipeline is crowded, but clinical validation still rests mainly on J&J.

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, only one addresses primary plasma cell leukaemia. What GPRC5D therapy can do in this aggressive plasma cell disease is still an open question.
  • No ADC or in vivo CAR programs appear among those tracked here. New formats or affinity tuning that reduce skin, nail and taste toxicity could be a way to stand apart from talquetamab.
  • No tracked trials reach primary completion in the next six months. Two questions remain open: whether GPRC5D expression or antigen loss after BCMA therapy can guide patient selection, and where single-target agents fit in earlier lines after MonumenTAL-6.
