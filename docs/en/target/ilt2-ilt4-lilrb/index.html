Target brief

Why it matters

LILRB1 (ILT2), LILRB2 (ILT4) and LILRB4 are inhibitory receptors on myeloid cells, NK and T cells. They bind HLA class I molecules such as HLA-G and signal through ITIMs, which keeps tumor-associated myeloid cells suppressive. Blocking them could repolarize macrophages and MDSCs and address myeloid resistance that PD-1 inhibitors leave untouched.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. All five programs tracked here are in Phase 1 or 1/2. No readouts or failures are logged, and no primary completions are due in the next six months. MSD's MK-4830 has run since 2019 across three trials, and its results are still not public.

Where the openings are

  • Blood cancers are thinly covered: among tracked programs, AML and multiple myeloma have one program each. LILRB4 is highly expressed on monocytic AML cells, which makes it a candidate for direct tumor targeting. No ADC or cell-therapy formats appear among programs tracked here.
  • Patient selection is not yet defined. One hypothesis to test is whether tumor HLA-G expression or the level of myeloid infiltration picks out responders, especially in solid tumors that existing programs already cover: NSCLC, esophageal squamous cell carcinoma and small cell lung cancer.
  • Multi-target designs are early. Mabwell's 6MW5311 is the only bispecific and was first registered in November 2026. There are no data yet on whether blocking ILT2 and ILT4 together, or building PD-(L)1 blockade into the same molecule, beats combining separate antibodies.
