Target brief

Why it matters

RAS genes (KRAS, NRAS, HRAS) are mutated in roughly a quarter of human cancers, and KRAS in over 90% of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. RAS-ON inhibitors bind the active, GTP-bound state and cover G12D, G12V, Q61 and other alleles that G12C-selective drugs miss. They may also blunt resistance driven by RAS reactivation.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • Among programs tracked here there are no readouts and no failures, and no trials reach primary completion in the next six months, so the clinical record is still thin. Daraxonrasib is the only phase 3 program. Most others are in phase 1 or 1/2, and no first real test is scheduled yet.

Where the openings are

  • Trials tracked here cluster in PDAC and unselected advanced solid tumors. CRC and NSCLC have one program each, and NRAS-driven tumors such as melanoma have none. All 30 trials are in oncology, which leaves non-oncology RASopathies open too.
  • ERAS-0015 is the only degrader among the nine programs tracked here. Whether degrading RAS gives deeper or longer-lasting pathway suppression than tri-complex or other inhibitors, at a tolerable dose, is still open. Preclinical and early clinical comparisons could answer it.
  • Pan-RAS agents also hit wild-type RAS in normal tissue, so the therapeutic window is the main question for later entrants. Ways to differ from daraxonrasib include selecting patients by allele (G12D, G12V, G12R, Q61) and co-mutation, treating after G12C-inhibitor resistance, and combining with upstream or downstream blockers.
