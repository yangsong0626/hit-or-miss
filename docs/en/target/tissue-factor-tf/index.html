Target brief

Why it matters

Tissue factor (TF, F3) binds FVIIa to start extrinsic coagulation and, through PAR2 signalling, drives tumour angiogenesis, invasion, metastasis and cancer-associated thrombosis. TF is overexpressed in cervical, pancreatic, head and neck, and non-small-cell lung cancers, and it internalises quickly once bound, which makes it an ADC target. Tisotumab vedotin is approved for recurrent cervical cancer.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record tracked here is still thin. The only readout, NCT03786081 (tisotumab vedotin plus bevacizumab and others in cervical cancer), was stopped early for the sponsor's strategic reasons, which says nothing against the mechanism. No tracked trials read out in the next six months, so there is no near-term test.

Where the openings are

  • The next-generation TF ADCs tracked here (Adcendo, Evopoint, Nanolattix, Sutro) are all in Phase 1, in NSCLC, pancreatic, colorectal and head and neck cancer. One hypothesis to test: whether new payloads or conjugation methods extend activity beyond cervical cancer.
  • Most tracked programs enrol broad solid-tumour populations. Whether TF expression (IHC) predicts response, and could be used to select patients, is still unproven. Answering that could set a newcomer apart from the leaders.
  • Nearly every tracked program is an ADC. None tracked here is a TF bispecific, CAR-T or radioconjugate. The stop of NCT03786081 also leaves open whether adding anti-angiogenic therapy helps.
