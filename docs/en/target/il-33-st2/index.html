Target brief

Why it matters

IL-33 is an epithelial alarmin released when airway cells are damaged by smoke, viruses or allergens. It signals through ST2 (IL1RL1) on ILC2s, mast cells and T cells, driving both type 2 and non-type 2 inflammation. GWAS link IL33 and IL1RL1 to asthma, and blocking this upstream node could reach COPD patients who are not eosinophilic.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • In COPD exacerbations, blocking either the ligand or the receptor has produced positive results: anti-ST2 astegolimab (NCT05595642, NCT05037929), anti-IL-33 itepekimab in AERIFY-1 (NCT04701983), and tozorakimab in OBERON and TITANIA, per AstraZeneca.
  • Results did not replicate reliably. Itepekimab's twin trial AERIFY-2 (NCT04751487) missed in its former-smoker cohort while AERIFY-1 met. That points to a modest or uneven effect and leaves open which COPD patients reliably benefit.
  • The evidence outside COPD is weaker. Itepekimab missed in a phase 2 bronchiectasis trial (NCT06280391). The CEREN-4 nasal polyp trial (NCT07484230) was stopped by sponsor decision, a business call that is not evidence against the mechanism.
  • The field is concentrated. All five programs tracked here are phase 3 monoclonal antibodies from three large companies, mostly in COPD, so differentiation is more likely to come from patient selection and dosing than from modality.

Where the openings are

  • The main open question is who responds. AERIFY-3 (NCT05326412) collected endobronchial biopsy signatures in former smokers. Tissue or blood markers that explain the AERIFY-1/-2 split could give a later entrant a way to differentiate. Itepekimab COPD trial NCT06208306, now past primary completion, may add evidence.
  • Three itepekimab phase 3 nasal polyp trials (NCT06834347, NCT06834360, NCT07424144) will test whether IL-33 blockade adds benefit in an indication already served by established type 2 biologics, regardless of the CEREN-4 stop.
  • Among programs tracked here, none use an inhaled format or a bispecific that pairs IL-33 with another epithelial cytokine. Asthma and severe viral lung infection have registered trials but no tracked readouts yet. Both remain untested openings.
