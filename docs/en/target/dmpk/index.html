Target brief

Why it matters

DM1 is caused by an expanded CTG repeat in the 3′ untranslated region of DMPK. The mutant transcript stays in the nucleus, traps MBNL splicing regulators and causes missplicing of genes such as CLCN1, which drives myotonia, weakness and cardiac conduction defects. Lowering toxic DMPK RNA in muscle has a clear genetic rationale. The hard part is getting oligonucleotides into muscle.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The record is thin. MARINA (NCT05027269) tested safety only, and Del-desiran's phase 3 HARBOR missed on hand-opening time, a myotonia measure. DYNE-101 has no readouts tracked here. Results from NCT06411288 and DYNE-101's phase 3 will be the next real tests.

Where the openings are

  • HARBOR's miss on hand-opening time could mean DMPK knockdown does not improve function, or the problem was the endpoint, dose or treatment length. Full NCT06411288 results showing changes in strength, timed function or splicing could help tell these apart.
  • Among programs tracked here, DYNE-101 is the only other late-stage DMPK program. Showing deeper or longer-lasting knockdown in muscle, linked to splicing correction, would help show whether HARBOR failed because of too little exposure or because of the mechanism.
  • No tracked program targets DM1's brain symptoms, cardiac conduction defects or the congenital form, and none uses repeat-targeting gene editing. Delivery to tissues beyond skeletal muscle is still an open question.
