Target brief

Why it matters

Nectin-4 (PVRL4) is a cell-adhesion molecule. It is highly expressed in urothelial carcinoma and often in breast, lung and other solid tumours, while normal-tissue expression is limited, mainly to skin and other epithelia. That profile suits ADC payload delivery. Enfortumab vedotin has been approved in urothelial cancer for years, which validates the target clinically.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • Efficacy evidence is concentrated in urothelial cancer and is moving into earlier disease. Perioperative enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab met its event-free survival endpoint in muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NCT03924895).
  • Some combination arms in NCT03869190, such as enfortumab vedotin plus niraparib, were closed early because the sponsor chose not to continue them. That was a business decision, not evidence against the mechanism or the combination.
  • Among programs tracked here, most follow-on programs are ADCs (7 of 12), with some bispecifics and cell therapies, and most are in phase 1 across solid tumours, NSCLC and breast cancer. No hypothesis-tested readout exists outside urothelial cancer yet. NCT04887870 reported safety data only, descriptively.

Where the openings are

  • Validation outside urothelial cancer is still open. TNBC and NSCLC each have three programs here but no readouts yet. One hypothesis to test: selecting patients by Nectin-4 expression level could raise response rates.
  • As enfortumab vedotin moves into the perioperative setting (NCT03924895), more patients will progress after EV. An open question is whether a different payload, a bicyclic toxin conjugate, a bispecific or a cell therapy can work after EV resistance.
  • CRB-701 has reached phase 3, while the Akeso and Rondo bispecifics and the NK and CAR cell therapies are early. The key question is whether any can keep efficacy while causing less skin toxicity and neuropathy. Program data have yet to answer this.
