Target brief

Why it matters

A GGGGCC hexanucleotide repeat expansion in intron 1 of C9orf72 is the most common genetic cause of both familial and sporadic ALS and FTD. It causes disease through three routes: loss of C9orf72 protein (which impairs autophagy and lysosomes), toxic repeat-RNA foci, and dipeptide-repeat proteins made by RAN translation. That gives a genetically defined patient group that can be clearly identified.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. This site tracks one program: IRulya's small molecule LAM-002A, in a Phase 2 ALS study (NCT05163886). The study enrolled 15 people, and its primary endpoints were safety and tolerability, with no formal hypothesis test, so it says nothing about efficacy. No tracked readout is due in the next six months to give a first real test.

Where the openings are

  • The only tracked program is a small molecule that acts on the downstream autophagy/lysosome pathway. No program tracked here targets the repeat RNA directly with ASOs, siRNA or gene editing. That approach could test the RNA-toxicity hypothesis.
  • CSF poly(GP) and other dipeptide-repeat biomarkers could be used to select patients and to measure drug effect. That would let small early studies confirm target engagement before moving to efficacy trials.
  • Among programs tracked here, none covers C9orf72-linked FTD or presymptomatic carriers. Because one mutation drives both ALS and FTD, there is room to expand into these indications.
