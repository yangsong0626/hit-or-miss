Target brief

Why it matters

TL1A (TNFSF15) signals through DR3 on T cells and innate lymphoid cells. It amplifies Th1, Th17 and Th2 inflammation and activates fibroblasts. GWAS link TNFSF15 variants to IBD, especially Crohn's disease, so blocking TL1A could treat inflammation and fibrosis at the same time.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The record is still thin. Tulisokibart was positive in UC induction (ATLAS-UC) and in a first dermatology Phase II, but there are no failures to compare against. The first real tests: afimkibart's UC Phase 3 trials (NCT06588855, NCT06589986), its atopic dermatitis trial (NCT06863961) and tulisokibart in RA (NCT07176390).

Where the openings are

  • Fibrosis is the least-tested part of the biology. Among programs tracked here, ILD and MASH have one program each, and none targets stricturing Crohn's. Fibrosis-focused endpoints could set TL1A apart from other IBD mechanisms.
  • It is still unclear whether TL1A blockade works outside the gut. The dermatology hit is a single signal. Afimkibart in atopic dermatitis (NCT06863961) and tulisokibart in RA (NCT07176390) will test whether the effect carries over to skin and joints.
  • All eight programs tracked here are monoclonal antibodies, and three are in Phase 3 for UC and Crohn's. Later entrants could set themselves apart with biomarker or genotype-based patient selection, longer half-life (as Vial's HLE program is trying), or bispecifics and combinations, which none of the tracked programs use yet.
