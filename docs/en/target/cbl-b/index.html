Target brief

Why it matters

CBL-B is an intracellular E3 ubiquitin ligase that sets the activation threshold of T and NK cells and enforces anergy when CD28 co-stimulation is absent. In CBL-B knockout mice, T cells activate without co-stimulation, reject tumors and resist suppressive signals such as TGF-β. Because the target sits inside the cell and is hard to reach with antibodies, it is a candidate small-molecule immune checkpoint.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record tracked here is still thin. There are two small-molecule programs: Nurix's NX-1607 in Phase 1 and HotSpot's HST-1011 in Phase 1/2. Neither has reported results, and no tracked trial reaches primary completion in the next six months, so efficacy and safety remain open questions.

Where the openings are

  • The tracked programs cover advanced solid tumors, ovarian cancer and gastric cancer. A testable question is whether selecting patients by T-cell infiltration or PD-1-refractory status shows where lowering the T-cell activation threshold matters most, especially in immune-cold tumors.
  • Knockout mice tend toward autoimmunity. Early data need to show the rate of immune-related adverse events and how pharmacodynamic markers such as peripheral T-cell activation or IL-2 track with dose. That will define how wide the therapeutic window is.
  • All tracked programs are systemic small molecules. One untested option is to delete CBL-B ex vivo, or pre-treat cells with a drug, in CAR-T or NK cell therapies, which could limit the risk of body-wide autoimmunity.
