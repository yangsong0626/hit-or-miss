Target brief

Why it matters

In RAS–MAPK signaling, BRAF and CRAF signal as dimers. First-generation BRAF V600 inhibitors cannot block dimers and can paradoxically activate the pathway. Pan-RAF inhibitors block both monomers and dimers. They are aimed at tumors driven by non-V600 BRAF mutations, BRAF fusions, or NRAS/KRAS mutations, such as pediatric low-grade glioma and low-grade serous ovarian cancer.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • All 8 programs tracked here are small molecules, and tovorafenib, avutometinib and naporafenib have reached phase 3. But no readouts or failures are recorded yet, so the clinical record is still thin. Avutometinib's KRAS-mutant trial NCT05669482 has passed its primary completion date, and its results will be the first real test tracked here.

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, indications cluster in melanoma, low-grade serous ovarian cancer and low-grade glioma. Common KRAS-mutant solid tumors get less coverage. One hypothesis to test is whether combining pan-RAF or RAF/MEK agents with direct KRAS inhibitors delays pathway reactivation.
  • Every program tracked here is a small-molecule inhibitor, and no other mechanism, such as RAF-dimer degraders, appears yet. Since glioma is a target indication, brain penetration and CNS exposure are another way to stand apart from the leading programs.
  • RAF alterations vary widely: non-V600 BRAF, fusions, NRAS and KRAS. Trials could test whether enrolling by alteration class identifies who benefits. NCT05669482 in KRAS-mutant patients will give the first reference point.
