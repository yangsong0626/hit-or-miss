Target brief

Why it matters

IL-1β is cleaved into its active form by the NLRP3 inflammasome and caspase-1, then signals through IL-1R1 and the co-receptor IL-1RAP to amplify neutrophil-driven inflammation. Gain-of-function inflammasome mutations cause autoinflammatory diseases such as CAPS. Anakinra, an IL-1 receptor antagonist, has been in clinical use for many years, so the pathway is well validated in humans.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • None of the IL-1β/IL-1R programs tracked here has a reported readout or failure yet, so the clinical record is still thin. The first real test is lutikizumab's Phase 3 trial in hidradenitis suppurativa (NCT06468228), which is expected to reach primary completion in December 2026.

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, hidradenitis suppurativa, Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis and atopic dermatitis each have just one program. NCT06468228 will test whether IL-1 blockade helps in neutrophil-rich barrier inflammation. The result could show whether these indications are worth pursuing on their own.
  • Most programs are injectable biologics. The only small molecule, HPB-092, is in Phase 1, and the gene therapy (FX201) and cell therapy programs are also early. For chronic diseases that need long-term dosing, oral or durable local delivery could set a program apart from the leading antibodies.
  • The site tracks three oncology trials, but none of the tracked programs lists a cancer indication. IL-1β drives tumor-promoting inflammation, so two hypotheses worth testing are selecting patients by inflammatory biomarkers and combining with immunotherapy. FMF already has two programs, and colchicine-inadequate responders could be a way to stratify patients.
