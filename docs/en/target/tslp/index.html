Target brief

Why it matters

TSLP is an epithelial alarmin released in response to allergens, viruses and pollutants. It sits upstream in the inflammatory cascade, activating dendritic cells, ILC2s and mast cells, and drives both type 2 and some non-type-2 inflammation. GWAS link the TSLP locus to asthma and allergy, and tezepelumab's approval in severe asthma validated the target clinically.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • Positive results now span several epithelial-barrier diseases. Verekitug met its primary endpoint in CRSwNP (NCT06164704), and tezepelumab met both co-primary endpoints in EoE in the CROSSING trial, so upstream blockade works beyond asthma.
  • Tezepelumab's OCS-sparing trial (NCT05398263) was scored as met but stopped early for slow recruitment, because maintenance oral steroid use is falling in practice. This was an operational problem, not a sign against the mechanism. Trials with OCS-reduction endpoints are getting harder to enroll.
  • Among programs tracked here, 12 companies run 12 programs: 9 monoclonal antibodies and 4 in Phase 3, including several Chinese companies. With so much overlap, competition is moving to bispecifics (e.g. Sanofi's lunsekimig), dosing interval and choice of indication.

Where the openings are

  • COPD has a fair number of programs but no readouts tracked here yet. It is still unknown which patients benefit (e.g. those with low eosinophils), so biomarker-based selection may decide the outcome.
  • Atopic dermatitis and EoE each have only 2 programs. ATI-045's AD trial (NCT07011706) is past its primary completion date with results pending, and will be the first test in skin disease.
  • Two Phase 3 CRSwNP readouts are coming (NCT06706817, NCT07107256). They will show whether TSLP blockade stands out in a field that already has biologics, and give a reference point for comparing bispecifics with single-target antibodies.
