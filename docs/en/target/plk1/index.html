Target brief

Why it matters

PLK1 is a serine/threonine kinase that controls mitotic entry, centrosome maturation, spindle assembly and cytokinesis, and it is overexpressed in many solid tumors. Preclinical work suggests KRAS-mutant cells are synthetically dependent on PLK1, which is the reason to test PLK1 inhibitors in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), a cancer dominated by KRAS mutations.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. Only one readout is tracked here: Cardiff Oncology's onvansertib plus nanoliposomal irinotecan in PDAC (NCT04752696). Its primary endpoint was ORR with no formal test. The sponsor ended it once enrollment and data were judged sufficient (coded operational), so it says nothing for or against the mechanism. No readouts are scheduled in the next six months.

Where the openings are

  • Only one PLK1 program is tracked here, and only in PDAC. Among programs tracked here, other KRAS-driven tumors such as colorectal or non-small cell lung cancer have none, which leaves open whether the synthetic-lethality idea holds outside the pancreas.
  • The stop note for NCT04752696 says the data will support future PDAC trials. Testing earlier lines of therapy, other chemotherapy backbones, and patient selection by KRAS variant or ctDNA dynamics are open hypotheses.
  • Only ATP-competitive small molecules are tracked here. Polo-box-domain binders or degraders could improve selectivity and widen the therapeutic window, and none appears among tracked programs.
