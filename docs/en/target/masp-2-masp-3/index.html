Target brief

Why it matters

MASP-2 is the effector serine protease of the lectin pathway: once recognition molecules bind pathogens or damaged endothelium, it cleaves C4 and C2 to start the complement cascade. MASP-3 activates pro-factor D, linking to the alternative pathway. Blocking MASP-2 shuts down the lectin pathway while sparing the classical pathway, a rationale for thrombotic microangiopathies driven by endothelial injury.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. This site tracks one program, Omeros' narsoplimab, a phase 2 antibody in transplant-associated thrombotic microangiopathy. It has no readouts or failures on record and no trials reach primary completion in the next six months, so no near-term readout will be a first real test.

Where the openings are

  • No program tracked here targets MASP-3 directly. Inhibiting MASP-3 to block factor D activation and dampen the alternative pathway is a separate hypothesis worth testing alongside MASP-2 inhibition.
  • Tracked programs cover only transplant-associated TMA. Lectin-pathway activation markers such as MBL levels or C4d deposition could be used to select patients in other complement diseases driven by endothelial injury.
  • The only modality tracked is an intravenous antibody. Whether an oral small molecule or a long-acting format can keep the lectin pathway suppressed in chronic indications is still an open question.
