Target brief

Why it matters

CD70, the TNF-family ligand for CD27, is normally found only on activated lymphocytes. It is overexpressed in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (driven by VHL loss and HIF) and on AML blasts and leukaemic stem cells, which makes it a tumour-selective target. Because it also marks activated autoreactive T and B cells, it is being extended to lupus and other autoimmune diseases.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. No readouts or failures are tracked here, and no trial reaches primary completion in the next six months. All 10 programs are in phase 1–2, 8 of them cell therapies. The most advanced is OncoVerity's antibody cusatuzumab (phase 2).

Where the openings are

  • Of the 10 tracked programs, 8 are cell therapies, one is an antibody, and none is an ADC or T-cell engager. CD70 is broadly expressed in ccRCC, so those formats could test the target without CAR-T fratricide (activated T cells also express CD70).
  • Three tracked trials are in autoimmune disease (SLE, myositis, juvenile dermatomyositis, polyarticular JIA). Whether depleting CD70+ activated lymphocytes adds anything to B-cell-directed therapy is still open. CD70+ T cells are elevated in lupus and could be used to select patients.
  • AML has only one program among those tracked. Hypomethylating agents raise CD70 on blasts, which supports combination regimens and targeting leukaemic stem cells. Measuring CD70 density before and after treatment could help with patient selection and dosing.
