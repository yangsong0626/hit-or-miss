Target brief

Why it matters

POLQ encodes Polθ, the key enzyme of microhomology-mediated (theta-mediated) end joining. It is barely expressed in normal tissue but upregulated in homologous recombination-deficient tumors, such as BRCA1/2-mutant cancers, which depend on it as a backup repair route. Blocking Polθ is synthetic lethal with HR deficiency and is proposed as a way to overcome PARP inhibitor resistance.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. Among programs tracked here there is one unnamed Danatlas small molecule in phase 1 for advanced solid tumors, with no readouts and no trials reaching primary completion in the next six months, so no near-term test of the mechanism is in view.

Where the openings are

  • Patient selection is the first open question: enriching for BRCA1/2 mutations, HRD scores or PARP inhibitor-resistant disease would test whether the synthetic-lethal rationale holds up in patients.
  • Polθ has both a helicase and a polymerase domain, and only one small molecule is tracked here. Inhibitors aimed at a specific domain, or other modalities such as degraders, could set a program apart.
  • No combination strategy is tracked yet. Pairing Polθ inhibition with PARP inhibitors, radiotherapy or other DNA-damaging agents may deepen synthetic lethality in HR-deficient tumors and is worth building into early trials.
