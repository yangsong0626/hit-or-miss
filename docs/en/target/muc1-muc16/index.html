Target brief

Why it matters

MUC1 is overexpressed and abnormally glycosylated in many adenocarcinomas, and its MUC1-C subunit acts as a cancer-driving signalling protein. MUC16, the source of the serum marker CA125, is highly expressed in epithelial ovarian cancer and helps tumours spread across the peritoneum by binding mesothelin. Both sit on the cell surface, but their outer domains shed, which makes antibody and cell-therapy targeting harder.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • Among programs tracked here, there is very little clinical evidence so far: seven programs, all in Phase 1 or 1/2, five of them cell therapies, and no readouts yet. No tracked trial reaches primary completion in the next six months, so the mechanism will not be tested soon.

Where the openings are

  • Five of the seven tracked programs are cell therapies, with just one ADC (Whitehawk) and one bispecific (NEOK Bio). Antibody drugs aimed at epitopes that stay on the cell, such as MUC1-C or the near-membrane region of MUC16, could test whether avoiding shed antigen improves drug exposure in the tumour.
  • Tracked programs focus on ovarian and breast cancer, with one in pancreatic cancer. MUC1 is also widely expressed in lung, gastric and colorectal adenocarcinoma, but no tracked program names these indications. They remain untested directions for MUC1-directed drugs.
  • The dual-targeting CAR-NK program EB-DNK101 could show whether hitting two targets helps overcome antigen heterogeneity in ovarian cancer. Selecting patients by tumour-specific glycoform expression, and using serum CA125 as a pharmacodynamic marker, could make early signals clearer.
