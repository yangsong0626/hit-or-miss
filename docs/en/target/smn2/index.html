Target brief

Why it matters

Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) is caused by loss of SMN1. Its paralog SMN2 differs by one base in exon 7, so that exon is usually skipped and little full-length SMN protein is made. SMN2 copy number tracks with disease severity. Splicing modulation that keeps exon 7 in is a validated strategy: nusinersen and risdiplam have both been approved for years.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record tracked here is still thin. There are only two programs (the ASO nusinersen and the small molecule risdiplam), with no tracked readouts or failures. The phase 3 nusinersen trial NCT04729907 was due to reach primary completion in July 2026, but no result is tracked here yet. It will be the first real test.

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, every agent is a splicing modifier aimed at SMN2 exon 7. No SMN-independent approaches (muscle-directed, neuroprotective) or SMN2 gene editing appear. Scholar Rock is listed on nusinersen trials, which hints that combination therapy is worth testing.
  • A testable hypothesis: biomarkers such as SMN2 copy number and neurofilament light could identify patients who respond poorly to splicing modifiers and guide dose escalation or switching.
  • The two drug classes are given intrathecally or as a daily oral dose. An open question is whether longer-acting, lower-burden formats could improve long-term benefit in adults and later-onset patients.
