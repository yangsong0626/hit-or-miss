Target brief

Why it matters

NLRP3 is a sensor inside the cell that detects danger signals such as urate crystals, cholesterol crystals and protein aggregates. Once activated, it drives caspase-1 to release IL-1β and IL-18. Gain-of-function NLRP3 mutations cause CAPS, and blocking IL-1β lowered cardiovascular events. This evidence supports developing oral inhibitors for gout, cardiometabolic disease and neurodegeneration.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. The only registry readout, NCT06771115 in obesity, had a safety primary endpoint and no formal efficacy test. The first real efficacy tests will come from NCT07220629 in obesity and NCT05658575 in acute gout flare.

Where the openings are

  • In acute gout flare, urate crystals activate NLRP3 directly, the shortest link from mechanism to disease. NCT05658575 is the only phase 2/3 program tracked here. It could show whether an oral small molecule works where the biology is clearest.
  • Obesity and cardiovascular risk is where most programs tracked here cluster. Selecting patients with high hsCRP or IL-6, or testing combinations with incretin drugs, may help a program stand apart from similar ones.
  • CAPS and related inherited autoinflammatory diseases have direct NLRP3 genetic validation, but no program tracked here targets them. Recurrent pericarditis (one program) and Parkinson's disease (two) also have little coverage.
