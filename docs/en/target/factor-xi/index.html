Target brief

Why it matters

Factor XI sits in the amplification loop of the intrinsic coagulation pathway. It drives thrombus growth more than it supports hemostasis. People born with FXI deficiency usually have mild bleeding but lower rates of venous thrombosis and ischemic stroke, which is genetic support for anticoagulation with less bleeding. The main uses are stroke prevention in atrial fibrillation (AF), VTE and cancer-associated thrombosis.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • This site tracks no FXI readouts or failures yet, so the clinical record is thin. The first real tests will be milvexian's two Phase 3 trials, in secondary stroke prevention (NCT05702034, at or near primary completion) and in AF (NCT05757869), plus REGN7508 in VTE (NCT07015905).

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, cancer-associated thrombosis and secondary stroke have one program each, while most programs focus on AF. Patients with high bleeding risk, such as those with cancer or on dialysis, are where a bleeding-sparing profile matters most and could set a program apart.
  • siRNA is only at Phase 1 (Sirnaomics, City). Infrequent dosing suits chronic AF anticoagulation, but long duration of effect raises open questions about reversal and peri-procedural management that trial design would need to address.
  • The upcoming milvexian readouts will give a first test of whether oral small-molecule FXIa inhibition keeps its efficacy while reducing bleeding. That will also inform the choice between antibodies (REGN7508, abelacimab) and oral drugs.

Coagulation · upstream Factor XII / Tissue factor (TF) · downstream FVIII mimetic (FIXa × FX)
Factor XII / Tissue factor (TF) → Factor XI / TFPI → FVIII mimetic (FIXa × FX) → Antithrombin
The contact path runs FXII → FXI; the extrinsic path starts with tissue factor and is held back by TFPI; both are amplified by FVIII/FIX into thrombin, which antithrombin shuts off. Haemophilia drugs rebalance via FVIII mimetics, TFPI or antithrombin.
