Target brief

Why it matters

Cholesterol 24-hydroxylase (CYP46A1) is a brain-specific enzyme that turns cholesterol into 24S-hydroxycholesterol, the main way the brain clears cholesterol. 24S-hydroxycholesterol enhances NMDA receptor activity, so blocking the enzyme was proposed as a way to calm glutamate-driven hyperexcitability in severe childhood epilepsies. In models of neurodegeneration, boosting the enzyme's activity looks protective.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The record is thin: only one program, Takeda's soticlestat, has reached Phase 3. Its open-label extension NCT05163314 was stopped because the pivotal SKYLINE and SKYWAY studies were negative, not for safety reasons. Among programs tracked here, no readouts are due in the next six months.

Where the openings are

  • It is still unclear whether the mechanism failed or the molecule did. Plasma 24S-hydroxycholesterol shows how well the drug hits its target. Linking how far it fell to seizure outcomes in existing data could show whether deeper or more brain-selective inhibition is worth testing.
  • No program tracked here tries the opposite approach: raising CYP46A1 activity, for example with gene therapy or activators, in Huntington's or Alzheimer's disease. Preclinical models suggest that faster cholesterol turnover in the brain is protective.
