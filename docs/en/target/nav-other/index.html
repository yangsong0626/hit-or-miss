Target brief

Why it matters

Nav1.1, Nav1.6 and Nav1.2 (SCN1A, SCN8A, SCN2A) set neuronal excitability in the brain, and variants in these genes cause developmental and epileptic encephalopathies. SCN1A loss of function weakens inhibitory interneurons and drives Dravet syndrome, so the aim there is to restore Nav1.1. SCN8A gain of function calls for selective Nav1.6 inhibition. Human genetics directly supports both approaches.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The record is still thin. The only readout is a Phase 2 of Neurocrine's Nav1.6 inhibitor NBI-921352 in SCN8A DEE (NCT05226780). It stopped after 8 patients on a sponsor decision, so it tells us nothing about efficacy. Zorevunersen's Phase 3 in Dravet (NCT06872125) will be the first real test.

Where the openings are

  • Now that NBI-921352 has stopped, no SCN8A DEE program remains among those tracked here. Nobody has yet shown whether selective Nav1.6 inhibition works in gain-of-function patients, and no program tracked here uses knockdown or allele-selective ASOs against SCN8A.
  • No Nav1.2 (SCN2A) programs are tracked here. Yet SCN2A variants split into gain-of-function infantile epilepsy and loss-of-function autism-like phenotypes. Sorting patients by the functional class of their variant could show whether to inhibit or upregulate the channel.
  • NCT06872125, with primary completion due in March 2027, should show whether raising Nav1.1 reduces seizures on top of standard care. Still unanswered: effects on cognition and behavior, the best age to treat, and delivery methods less invasive than repeated intrathecal dosing.
