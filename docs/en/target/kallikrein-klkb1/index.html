Target brief

Why it matters

KLKB1 encodes plasma kallikrein, which cleaves high-molecular-weight kininogen to release bradykinin. When C1 inhibitor is deficient, this pathway runs unchecked and drives the recurrent swelling attacks of hereditary angioedema (HAE). Kallikrein inhibitors such as lanadelumab and berotralstat are approved for HAE, so the target is well validated. The open questions are how the drug is given and how well attacks stay controlled over the long term.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record here is still thin. All 3 programs tracked here are in Phase 3, but no readouts or failures are logged yet. The first real test will be donidalorsen's Phase 3 trial NCT05392114, with primary completion expected in December 2026.
  • Every program tracked here targets HAE. The programs compete on how convenient prevention is (a daily oral pill versus an antisense drug injected under the skin at long intervals), not on whether the mechanism works.

Where the openings are

  • All 9 trials tracked here are in HAE. Other bradykinin-driven conditions are open hypotheses for expanding beyond HAE: HAE with normal C1-INH, angioedema caused by ACE inhibitors, and diabetic macular edema.
  • No gene-editing programs appear among those tracked here, and there is only one pediatric HAE entry. That leaves room for one-time or very long-interval KLKB1 silencing and for pediatric dosing studies.
  • Plasma kallikrein activity or cleaved HMWK could serve as markers of drug effect to fit dose and dosing interval to each patient. NCT05392114 should show whether long-interval antisense dosing keeps attacks steadily controlled.
