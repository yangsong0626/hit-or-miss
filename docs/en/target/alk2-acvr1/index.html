Target brief

Why it matters

ACVR1 encodes ALK2, a BMP type I receptor. The gain-of-function R206H mutation causes nearly all classic FOP: the mutant receptor responds abnormally to activin A and drives heterotopic ossification, giving strong genetic validation. Somatic ACVR1 mutations also occur in DIPG, and the ALK2–hepcidin axis is linked to anemia of inflammation.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. This site tracks only one program, a Regeneron Phase 3 antibody in FOP registered in April 2026. It has no readouts, and no trial reaches primary completion in the next six months, so there is no near-term test of the mechanism.

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, the only modality is an antibody. Small-molecule ALK2 kinase inhibitors and approaches that selectively hit the mutant receptor are absent. Preclinical work suggests some ACVR1 antibodies can activate the mutant receptor, so comparing modalities is worth exploring.
  • FOP is the only indication tracked here. ACVR1-mutant DIPG and hepcidin-driven anemia have a biological rationale but no tracked programs, which makes them candidate hypotheses for expansion.
  • FOP progresses through episodic flare-ups and slow ossification. Whether imaging-measured new heterotopic bone volume and flare frequency hold up as endpoints will decide how clearly the Regeneron Phase 3 study can answer its question.
