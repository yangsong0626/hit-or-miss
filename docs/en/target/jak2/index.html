Target brief

Why it matters

JAK2 is the non-receptor tyrosine kinase that carries signals from the EPO, TPO and other cytokine receptors. The acquired V617F mutation, found in most polycythemia vera and about half of essential thrombocythemia and primary myelofibrosis cases, locks JAK–STAT signaling on. Ruxolitinib has been approved in myelofibrosis for years, which validated the target.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The 4 programs tracked here (3 in Phase 3, 1 in Phase 1) have no readouts and no recorded stops or failures. No trials reach primary completion in the next six months, so the clinical record is too thin to draw lessons from yet.

Where the openings are

  • Both Phase 3 recombinant-protein programs (AOP Orphan, PharmaEssentia) target essential thrombocythemia. Among programs tracked here, none lists polycythemia vera as its indication, which is a gap worth testing.
  • Current JAK2 inhibitors also hit wild-type JAK2, which commonly causes anemia and thrombocytopenia. Whether a V617F mutant-selective inhibitor can avoid these toxicities is still open. Eli Lilly's LY5830966 is the only early small molecule tracked here, and its selectivity has not been disclosed.
  • Only one Phase 3 program (Bristol Myers Squibb) tracked here addresses MPN-associated anemia. Using JAK2 V617F allele burden to stratify patients or as a disease-modification endpoint could help programs set themselves apart.
