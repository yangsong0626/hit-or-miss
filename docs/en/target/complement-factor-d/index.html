Target brief

Why it matters

Factor D is the serine protease that cleaves factor B within C3bB. This is the rate-limiting step of the alternative complement pathway and its amplification loop. Because plasma levels of factor D are very low, an oral small molecule can inhibit it. Overactivity of the alternative pathway drives the C3-mediated extravascular hemolysis that persists in PNH under C5 blockade, and it contributes to C3 glomerulopathy and IgA nephropathy.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The record is thin. The only tracked readouts are two phase 2 trials of BioCryst's BCX9930, in PNH (NCT05116787) and C3G (NCT05162066). Both stopped by sponsor decision, a business reason that says nothing against the mechanism. The first real test will be danicopan's phase 3 PNH trial, NCT05389449. Its results are not yet tracked here.

Where the openings are

  • Kidney disease is largely open. Among tracked programs, the only factor D trial in C3G or IgA nephropathy was BCX9930's, which stopped for business reasons (NCT05162066). No one has yet shown whether oral factor D blockade lowers proteinuria in C3G.
  • NCT05389449 will add phase 3 data on danicopan in PNH. Open questions for the class: does factor D inhibition work best added to C5 blockade or on its own? Can C3 deposition on red cells pick out patients with residual extravascular hemolysis?
  • Factor D levels are low but turnover is fast, so efficacy depends on sustained, near-complete inhibition. Only two small molecules are tracked. Longer-acting compounds, or other modalities that keep suppression steady between doses, are an untried way to differentiate. Meningococcal infection risk needs monitoring.
