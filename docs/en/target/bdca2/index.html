Target brief

Why it matters

BDCA2 (CD303) is a C-type lectin receptor found only on plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs). Antibody binding suppresses TLR7/9-driven type I interferon production. Type I IFN drives SLE and cutaneous lupus, and the approval of the IFNAR blocker anifrolumab validated that axis. Targeting BDCA2 aims to shut down interferon at its source.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record tracked here is still thin. There are no registry readouts and no failures. The only data point is a positive Biogen press release on 52-week Phase 2 data from the ongoing Phase 2/3 AMETHYST study. The first real tests are three litifilimab trials: NCT04961567 and NCT04895241 in SLE, and NCT05531565 in subacute cutaneous lupus. All have passed primary completion, and results are pending.

Where the openings are

  • pDCs are abundant in cutaneous lupus lesions. One hypothesis is that BDCA2 antibodies work better in skin-dominant lupus than in systemic SLE. Comparing NCT05531565 with the two SLE Phase 3 trials should show which patients the mechanism suits best.
  • Only 3 programs are tracked here, and Biogen is well ahead. The followers, Sunshine Guojian (Phase 2) and Biocad's BCD-256 (Phase 1), could set themselves apart by selecting patients with an interferon gene signature.
  • Other pDC- and interferon-driven diseases, such as dermatomyositis and Sjögren's, have strong biological rationale. No BDCA2 programs for them are tracked here.
