Target brief

Why it matters

DPP1 (cathepsin C) activates neutrophil elastase, PR3 and cathepsin G while neutrophils mature in the bone marrow. Inhibiting it reduces the activity of all three at the source, which may limit airway damage in bronchiectasis and COPD. Loss-of-function CTSC mutations cause Papillon-Lefèvre syndrome without obvious systemic immunodeficiency. This genetic evidence suggests long-term inhibition can be tolerated.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record tracked here is still thin, with no readouts or failures so far. The first real test is NCT06660992, Haisco's Phase 3 trial of HSK31858 in non-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis. Its primary completion is due in late November 2026.

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, COPD and asthma have only a few Phase 2 programs. Whether DPP1 inhibition helps beyond bronchiectasis is untested. Selecting patients by sputum elastase activity or a neutrophil-high phenotype is a testable hypothesis.
  • Onchilles' N17350 is the only recombinant-enzyme program, tested in triple-negative breast cancer and solid tumors. It inverts the respiratory logic by using elastase to kill tumor cells. Whether early data support this mechanism is an open question.
  • All tracked respiratory programs are small-molecule DPP1 inhibitors. One way to stand apart is to reduce the skin hyperkeratosis and periodontal effects seen in PLS. Another is to enter neutrophil-driven diseases that no program here addresses.
