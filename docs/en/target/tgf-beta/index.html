Target brief

Why it matters

TGF-β1 turns fibroblasts into myofibroblasts and drives matrix deposition and epithelial-mesenchymal transition. That makes it a core pathway in IPF and other fibrotic diseases. In tumors it also suppresses immunity and keeps T cells out. Pirfenidone, approved for IPF, works in part by dampening TGF-β signaling.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record tracked here is thin. All three readouts are for STP705: NCT04669808 and NCT05421013 had no formal test, and NCT05196373 was cancelled before enrolling anyone. These stops were for business or unstated reasons, so they say nothing against the mechanism. The first real tests will be NCT06241560 (IPF) and NCT07388680 (radiation-induced lung injury).

Where the openings are

  • Seven of the nine programs tracked here are small molecules, mostly new formulations or generics of pirfenidone. No ligand-blocking antibodies or traps appear. Given the toxicity concerns with blocking TGF-β throughout the body, local delivery or blocking only the activation step (integrin αvβ6, GARP) is worth testing.
  • Few programs go beyond IPF: radiation-induced lung injury, immune-related pneumonitis and pneumoconiosis have one program each. NCT07388680 will show whether pirfenidone works in radiation lung injury. If it does, immune-related pneumonitis is a logical next test.
  • STP705 knocks down TGF-β1 locally, but its data in BCC and isSCC are descriptive only, and the program was scaled back for business reasons. Whether local TGF-β1 silencing clears lesions on histology is still open. Selecting patients by TGF-β pathway activity in the lesion could sharpen a future test.
