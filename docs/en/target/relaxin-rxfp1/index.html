Target brief

Why it matters

RXFP1 is the GPCR for the peptide hormone relaxin-2. Acting through cAMP and nitric oxide, it dilates blood vessels, raises renal blood flow and glomerular filtration, and dampens fibrosis and inflammation. It drives the kidney's adaptation to pregnancy. That profile fits settings where kidney perfusion drops suddenly, such as acute kidney injury after cardiac surgery.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • This site tracks only one program: a Phase 2 program from River 2 Renal (drug and modality not disclosed), first posted June 2026, in acute kidney injury after cardiac and valve surgery. It has no readouts, and no primary completions are due in the next six months.

Where the openings are

  • No long-acting relaxin analogue, small-molecule RXFP1 agonist or agonist antibody is tracked here. A short infusion may suit perioperative AKI, but chronic uses such as kidney or cardiac fibrosis would need a durable, selective agonist. Signalling bias is a key design question.
  • Enrolling on clinical risk alone can dilute an effect. A testable idea: enrich with kidney-stress biomarkers such as urinary TIMP-2·IGFBP7 and dose before bypass. The vasodilatory and antifibrotic actions should then work before the injury sets in.
  • No programs are tracked here in chronic kidney disease, HFpEF with renal congestion, liver or lung fibrosis, or preeclampsia. When the River 2 Renal trials eventually report renal blood flow or GFR data, those results could help show which of these is worth pursuing.
