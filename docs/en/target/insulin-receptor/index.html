Target brief

Why it matters

The insulin receptor is a receptor tyrosine kinase. When insulin binds, IRS–PI3K–AKT signalling drives glucose uptake in muscle and fat and suppresses glucose output from the liver. Type 1 diabetes lacks insulin, and type 2 combines too little insulin with resistance to it, so agonising this receptor is the best-validated way to lower glucose. Current work focuses on stretching basal insulin dosing to once a week.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. This site has only two Phase 3 readouts, both for Novo Nordisk's insulin icodec in type 2 diabetes (NCT06340854, NCT05813912). Both met their HbA1c primary endpoint, and no failures or early stops are recorded. The real new tests are GZR4's Phase 3 NCT06767735 (primary completion 2026-08, results pending) and icodec in type 1 diabetes, NCT07076199 (2027-01).

Where the openings are

  • Type 1 diabetes is still unproven: all of icodec's tracked hits come from type 2. NCT07076199 will test whether weekly dosing holds up in patients with higher hypoglycaemia risk and frequent dose changes. Hypoglycaemia and titration rules may decide who stands out.
  • Among programs tracked here, GZR4 is the only other weekly insulin in Phase 3. How much room later entrants have will depend on whether NCT06767735 shows a difference in hypoglycaemia rates, ease of titration, or fit with GLP-1-based therapy.
  • All three programs are peptide or protein hormones. None tracked here is a glucose-responsive insulin, an oral form, or an agonist antibody to the receptor. None targets the severe insulin-resistance syndromes caused by INSR mutations. Each is open as a research hypothesis.
