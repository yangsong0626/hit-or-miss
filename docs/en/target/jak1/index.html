Target brief

Why it matters

JAK1 partners with JAK2, JAK3 or TYK2 to signal for many inflammatory cytokines, including IL-6, interferons, IL-4/IL-13 and IL-31. Selective JAK1 inhibition aims to block these pathways while sparing JAK2-dependent blood-cell signaling. Approved drugs such as upadacitinib have validated the mechanism in rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease and atopic dermatitis.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • Of the readouts tracked here, only STOP-HS2 (NCT05620836) tested a hypothesis, and povorcitinib met its primary endpoint in hidradenitis suppurativa. The other two were descriptive. The next real tests are povorcitinib in prurigo nodularis (NCT06516965, NCT06516952) and upadacitinib in lupus (NCT05843643).

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, interferon-driven and vasculitic diseases get little coverage: one Takayasu arteritis program, two cGVHD programs and a single lupus trial (NCT05843643). That trial could show whether JAK1 selectivity is enough to control type I interferon-driven disease, and could support selecting patients by interferon signature.
  • All eight tracked programs are oral small molecules that act systemically. No topical or gut-restricted JAK1 inhibitors appear here. Such drugs could test whether local exposure keeps efficacy in skin or bowel disease while avoiding the JAK class's systemic safety concerns, which FINCH 4 (NCT03025308) is following long term.
  • Povorcitinib is moving into skin diseases beyond atopic dermatitis, which already has three programs here. The prurigo nodularis trials (NCT06516965, NCT06516952) and a second HS trial (NCT06212999) will show whether the STOP-HS2 result replicates, and whether itch- and neutrophil-driven skin disease is a separate opening.
