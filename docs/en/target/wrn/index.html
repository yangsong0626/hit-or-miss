Target brief

Why it matters

WRN is a RecQ-family DNA helicase. In microsatellite-unstable (MSI-H/dMMR) tumors, expanded TA-dinucleotide repeats form secondary structures that need WRN to unwind them. Without WRN these cells accumulate chromosome breaks and die, while microsatellite-stable cells are largely spared. Several genome-wide CRISPR screens confirmed this synthetic lethality, which spans MSI-H subsets of colorectal, endometrial and gastric cancer.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. All five programs tracked here are in phase 1 to 2 and mostly small molecules. None has read out, and no trial reaches primary completion in the next six months. Whether the synthetic lethality carries over to patients is still open.

Where the openings are

  • Most programs enroll broad 'advanced solid tumors', and only a few restrict to MSI-H/dMMR. A finer biomarker than MSI status, such as TA-repeat expansion burden, might better predict who benefits.
  • MSI-H tumors often respond to PD-1 blockade, so patients who progress on PD-1 therapy are a key open population. Two questions to test: WRN inhibitor monotherapy in that later-line setting, or earlier use in combination with PD-1.
  • Among programs tracked here, only one names colorectal cancer. No program specifically targets MSI-H endometrial or gastric cancer. If resistance mutations in WRN itself emerge, molecules that bind differently (covalent vs non-covalent) could set programs apart.
