Target brief

Why it matters

FLT3 is a receptor tyrosine kinase on hematopoietic progenitors. About a third of AML cases carry activating FLT3-ITD or TKD mutations, and ITD predicts relapse and poor outcome. Approvals of midostaurin and gilteritinib validated the target. FLT3 is also expressed on the surface of most AML blasts, which makes it an antigen for bispecifics and CAR-T.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record here is still thin. This site tracks no FLT3 readouts, failures or early stops, and no trials reach primary completion in the next six months, so there is no upcoming test to watch. Of the 10 programs, most are phase 1 small molecules. Only quizartinib is in phase 3.

Where the openings are

  • Few programs use something other than a small molecule. Among programs tracked here, only the CLN-049 bispecific and Hemogenyx's CAR-T do, both early. Because they target surface FLT3 rather than the mutation, they might reach FLT3-wild-type AML. The open question is how much they damage normal progenitors and dendritic cells.
  • The quizartinib program is co-listed with Kura Oncology, and the conditions include NPM1-mutated and MLL-rearranged leukemia. That points to FLT3-plus-menin-pathway combinations as a hypothesis worth testing, with patients selected by co-mutation status.
  • Newer small molecules such as HPB-092 and Zhejiang ACEA's compound might stand apart by covering F691L gatekeeper and D835-type TKD resistance mutations, or by dual-target designs like GB3226. Patients who relapse after earlier FLT3 inhibitors are a natural population to start in.
