Target brief

Why it matters

KRAS G12D is the most common KRAS mutation in cancer. It is frequent in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma and also occurs in colorectal and lung cancers. The mutation locks KRAS in its GTP-bound active state, which keeps MAPK signalling switched on. G12D lacks the cysteine that G12C inhibitors such as sotorasib bind to, so it needs different chemistry, and clinical candidates have only appeared recently.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. This site tracks 13 programs, four of them in Phase 3 (RMC-9805 and programs from Astellas, Incyte and Genfleet). None has a readout or failure yet, and no tracked trial reaches primary completion in the next six months.

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, NSCLC is the indication listed most often. Pancreatic cancer, where G12D is most common, is spread across several separate labels. Designs built for PDAC, especially in earlier lines or around surgery, may be under-covered given the biology.
  • Eleven of the 13 tracked programs are small molecules. The only exceptions are AstraZeneca's autologous NT-112 and one antibody-labeled GFH375 arm. Cell therapies or vaccines aimed at the G12D neoantigen in HLA-matched patients are rare here and could complement inhibitors.
  • With four Phase 3 programs running in parallel, differentiation may depend on questions no tracked readout has answered yet: whether hitting the active or the inactive state of KRAS works better, which co-mutations predict benefit, and which partner drugs slow adaptive resistance, such as EGFR feedback in colorectal cancer.
