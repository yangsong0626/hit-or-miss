Target brief

Why it matters

HLA-G is a non-classical MHC class Ib molecule, normally confined largely to the maternal-fetal interface. It binds ILT2, ILT4 and KIR2DL4 to suppress T, NK and myeloid cells. Many solid tumors re-express HLA-G to evade immunity while normal adult tissues express little, making it both a checkpoint-blockade target and a tumor-selective antigen for cell therapy.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The three programs tracked here are all in phase 1 or 1/2, with no readouts, failures or early stops recorded and no trials reaching primary completion in the next six months. The record is still thin: TTX-080 entered phase 1 in 2020 but no results are tracked, so human proof of concept for HLA-G blockade is still missing.

Where the openings are

  • Programs tracked here comprise two monoclonal antibodies and one CAR.BiTE γδ T-cell therapy. ADCs and T-cell-engaging bispecifics, which would exploit tumor-selective HLA-G expression, are not represented and could be explored.
  • All tracked programs enroll broad solid-tumor populations. Selecting patients by tumor HLA-G expression or soluble HLA-G, and focusing on high-expressing tumor types, may give a cleaner test of the mechanism.
  • Blocking the ligand and blocking the ILT2/ILT4 receptors cover different biology. Combination with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors and activity in PD-1-refractory patients remain open questions.
