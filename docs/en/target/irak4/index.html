Target brief

Why it matters

IRAK4 is the core kinase and scaffold of the Myddosome downstream of TLRs and IL-1 family receptors, driving NF-κB and other inflammatory signaling. MYD88 L265P mutations drive some B-cell lymphomas through this pathway. People born without IRAK4 are prone to pyogenic bacterial infections, mainly in childhood, but are otherwise largely healthy. That suggests blocking IRAK4 may have a reasonable safety margin.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. Two Phase 2 small-molecule programs are tracked here (AstraZeneca, Leadingtac). Neither has a readout or a recorded failure, and no tracked trial reaches primary completion in the next six months, so no near-term data will test the mechanism.

Where the openings are

  • Tracked trials cover COPD, atopic dermatitis and blood cancers. In blood cancers, selecting patients by MYD88 L265P or related mutations may be the most direct test of whether the tumors depend on this pathway.
  • All tracked programs are small molecules, but the facts do not say which are kinase inhibitors and which are degraders. IRAK4 works as both a kinase and a scaffold, so whether a drug blocks both functions may be the key way to stand out.
  • No tracked program covers other IL-1/TLR-driven diseases such as hidradenitis suppurativa, rheumatoid arthritis or lupus. With long-term dosing, bacterial infection risk also deserves systematic monitoring.
