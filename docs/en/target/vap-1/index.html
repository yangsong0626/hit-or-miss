Target brief

Why it matters

VAP-1 (AOC3/SSAO) is an endothelial adhesion molecule with amine oxidase activity. It mediates leukocyte adhesion and transmigration, and its reaction products (H2O2, aldehydes) add oxidative and glycation stress. Soluble VAP-1 is elevated in diabetes, and retinal leukostasis is thought to drive early microvascular damage in diabetic retinopathy.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record tracked here is still thin: one Phase 2 small molecule from Vantage in diabetic retinopathy/NPDR, with no readouts yet and no trials reaching primary completion in the next six months. The first human test of the mechanism will be that Phase 2 readout, which falls outside the tracked window.

Where the openings are

  • Most NPDR patients are not yet candidates for intravitreal injections, so an oral anti-inflammatory, anti-adhesion drug could fill an early-intervention gap. The key open question is whether it shows a signal on DRSS improvement or slower progression.
  • Soluble VAP-1 levels or retinal inflammation imaging could be tested for patient selection and as pharmacodynamic markers, which would improve the odds of detecting an effect in small Phase 2 trials.
  • No VAP-1 program tracked here targets anything outside ophthalmology. Given VAP-1's role in leukocyte recruitment and fibrosis, liver fibrosis or chronic inflammatory diseases are hypothetical directions for expansion.
