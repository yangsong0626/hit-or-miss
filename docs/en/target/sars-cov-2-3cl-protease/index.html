Target brief

Why it matters

3CL protease (Mpro) cuts viral polyproteins into the proteins the virus needs to replicate, so blocking it halts SARS-CoV-2 replication. Its active site is highly conserved across coronaviruses and has no close human counterpart, which makes selective inhibitors feasible. Nirmatrelvir given with ritonavir has shown the mechanism works clinically in high-risk COVID-19.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The record tracked here is thin: one Phase 3 readout for S-217622 (Shionogi). SCORPIO-HR (NCT05305547, n=2093) missed its primary endpoint, time to sustained symptom resolution. No trials reach primary completion in the next six months, so there will be no further test soon.

Where the openings are

  • NCT05305547 missed on a symptom-resolution endpoint. One hypothesis to test: 3CL inhibition may show clearer benefit in groups where ongoing viral replication drives disease, such as immunocompromised patients with prolonged shedding, if measured with virological or hospitalization endpoints.
  • Among programs tracked here, the field is one small molecule from one company. Because the active site is conserved, no program in this dataset yet covers pan-coronavirus inhibitors for future pandemics or combinations with polymerase inhibitors to limit resistance.
  • Enrolling patients by time since symptom onset or by baseline viral load could test whether the benefit sits mainly in early, high-replication patients. A single trial that missed a symptom endpoint cannot answer this.
