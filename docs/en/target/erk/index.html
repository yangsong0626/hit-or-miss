Target brief

Why it matters

ERK1/2 are the last kinases in the RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK cascade and control cell proliferation and survival. In tumors, ERK reactivation is a common way cancers become resistant to BRAF and MEK inhibitors, so ERK is a downstream choke point. VEGFR2 signaling also drives endothelial cell growth partly through ERK, which is the reason to test it in wet AMD.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. Among programs tracked here, the only one is Filamon's small-molecule BETA-TT8, in a phase 1/2 wet AMD trial first posted in August 2026. There are no readouts or failures yet, and no trial reaches primary completion in the next six months.

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, no ERK program is in oncology, where the biology is strongest: ERK reactivation after BRAF/MEK inhibition, and RAS-driven tumors. Whether a direct ERK inhibitor, alone or in combination, can overcome that resistance is still an open question.
  • In wet AMD, the open question is how ERK inhibition fits next to standard anti-VEGF care. Can it add benefit in patients who respond poorly, or extend dosing intervals? MEK inhibitors are known to cause serous retinopathy, so local eye delivery and close retinal monitoring seem prudent.
  • Pharmacodynamic markers such as phosphorylated ERK, or readouts of downstream gene activity, could confirm early that the drug hits its target and help select patients. That matters for a target with no efficacy data tracked so far.
