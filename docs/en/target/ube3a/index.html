Target brief

Why it matters

UBE3A encodes an E3 ubiquitin ligase. In neurons only the maternal copy is expressed, because the paternal copy is silenced by the antisense transcript UBE3A-ATS. Losing maternal UBE3A causes Angelman syndrome. Knocking down UBE3A-ATS with an ASO can switch the paternal copy back on, and every program tracked here uses that approach.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • In the Phase 3 Aspire trial (NCT06617429), Ultragenyx's apazunersen missed both its primary endpoint, a Bayley-4 cognitive score, and its key secondary endpoints. It is the first controlled Phase 3 readout for this mechanism tracked here, and it was negative.
  • After Aspire, Ultragenyx stopped two other apazunersen trials on the same day for lack of efficacy: Phase 2 Aurora (NCT07157254) and a Phase 3 safety study (NCT06415344). Both had safety primary endpoints, so the stops mean the program was dropped, not that a new safety signal appeared.
  • All the negative evidence comes from one molecule. Obudanersen (Ionis) and rugonersen (OHB Pediatrics) are still in Phase 3, so it is not yet clear the failure applies to the whole UBE3A-ATS knockdown approach. No readout tracked here is due in the next six months to settle that.

Where the openings are

  • Whether obudanersen and rugonersen avoid Aspire's outcome may depend on how they differ from apazunersen in dose, CNS distribution, patient age and endpoints. One hypothesis to test: treating earlier, following patients longer, or using an endpoint other than the Day 338 Bayley-4 cognitive score may detect benefit more easily.
  • Trials could split patients by genotype (deletion, UBE3A point mutation, UPD). They could also use biomarkers such as EEG delta power to check that the paternal copy is actually switched on. That would show whether a failure means the drug missed its target or that hitting it brought no benefit.
  • All three programs tracked here are ASOs. Among programs tracked here, there are no clinical programs for UBE3A gene replacement, editing of UBE3A-ATS, or small molecules that switch the paternal copy back on. These are possible ways to stand apart from the ASOs.
