Target brief

Why it matters

CDC7 pairs with DBF4 to form DDK, which phosphorylates the MCM2-7 helicase to fire replication origins, a key switch for starting DNA replication. Cancer cells under high replication stress depend on it and tend to die when it is inhibited, while normal cells mostly pause reversibly. That suggests a usable therapeutic window for a cell-cycle target.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. Among programs tracked here, the only one is an unnamed Phase 1/2 program from Lin in relapsed or refractory acute leukaemias. There are no readouts, no failures, and no trials reaching primary completion in the next six months. That trial's results will be the first real test.

Where the openings are

  • The only program tracked here is in acute leukaemias. Solid tumours with high replication stress, such as TP53-mutant or MYC-driven cancers, have no tracked programs and could test how far CDC7 dependence extends.
  • Replication-stress biomarkers such as MCM2 phosphorylation or TP53 status could be tested as a way to pick the patients most likely to respond.
  • Whether CDC7 inhibitors work better alongside DNA-damaging chemotherapy, ATR inhibitors or standard leukaemia regimens is untested among tracked programs. The same goes for other modalities such as degraders.
