Target brief

Why it matters

CD38 is an NAD-hydrolysing ectoenzyme expressed at high levels on myeloma plasma cells and on the plasmablasts and long-lived plasma cells that make pathogenic antibodies. Anti-CD38 antibodies clear these cells through ADCC, CDC, phagocytosis and immunomodulation. Daratumumab and isatuximab are established in multiple myeloma, and development is now moving into antibody-driven autoimmune and renal disease.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. Both tracked readouts lacked a formal hypothesis test: NCT07217184 reported isatuximab dose-expansion safety, and NCT01946477 reported daratumumab ORR descriptively. No failures or early stops are recorded. The first real tests will be felzartamab's Phase 3 in antibody-mediated rejection (NCT06685757) and isatuximab's Phase 3 in plasma cell myeloma (NCT03319667).

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, antibody-mediated disease is still open. AMR, PMN, IgAN, gMG and Graves' disease each have only 1-2 programs, mostly felzartamab and mezagitamab. A testable hypothesis: selecting patients by donor-specific antibodies or anti-PLA2R titres could make the benefit of plasma-cell depletion easier to see.
  • Monoclonal antibodies make up 11 of the 17 tracked programs, with one ADC (STI-6129) and one cell therapy. It is unproven whether bispecifics, ADCs, or CAR-T designs that avoid fratricide of CD38-positive T cells add benefit in myeloma that progresses after antibody therapy.
  • NCT06685757 will be the first Phase 3 test of a CD38 antibody outside myeloma. A positive result would quickly crowd the autoimmune and transplant space, so later entrants may need to stand out on route of administration, infection risk from NK-cell depletion, or how long remissions last.
