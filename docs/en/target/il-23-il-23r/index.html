Target brief

Why it matters

IL-23 (p19 plus p40) signals through IL-23R, JAK2/TYK2 and STAT3 to sustain pathogenic Th17 cells that produce IL-17 and IL-22. IL23R variants are linked to inflammatory bowel disease and psoriasis, which gives the target genetic support. Ustekinumab (p40), guselkumab and risankizumab (p19) have been approved in psoriasis for years, so the clinical validation is solid.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • Psoriasis is where the target is most proven. The oral peptide IL-23R antagonist JNJ-77242113 met its primary endpoint in NCT06095115, NCT06143878 and NCT06220604, which shows that oral dosing can reproduce antibody-level efficacy.
  • The evidence now reaches hard-to-treat sites and specific populations: JNJ-77242113 met its endpoint in scalp and genital disease (ICONIC-TOTAL, NCT06095102), and so did guselkumab in VISIBLE (NCT05272150).
  • IBD has the most programs tracked here (9 in Crohn's disease, 7 in ulcerative colitis), but the recent IBD readouts had no formal hypothesis test: the pediatric ustekinumab studies NCT04630028 and NCT04673357 were descriptive.
  • The only failure tracked here is brazikumab (NCT03759288), which was stopped for strategic reasons. That says nothing against the mechanism and more about the commercial pressure late entrants face against established p19 drugs. No trial tracked here was stopped for safety.

Where the openings are

  • Among readouts tracked here, nobody has yet tested whether oral IL-23R blockade gets enough drug to the gut to work. NCT06807424 will test JNJ-77242113 in psoriatic arthritis, but IBD is still an open question for this drug.
  • Fistulizing Crohn's disease has only one tracked program (guselkumab, NCT05347095). Together with NCT06063967 (risankizumab, Crohn's) and NCT06260163 (guselkumab, ulcerative colitis), it may show how far p19 drugs can go in refractory patients.
  • p19 antibodies are increasingly alike. One hypothesis is to select patients by IL23R variant or by IL-22/Th17 biomarkers; another is to combine IL-23 blockade with other mechanisms in refractory IBD. Results from the Phase 2 mirikizumab ulcerative colitis trial (NCT06598943) are still pending.
