Target brief

Why it matters

IL-31, made mainly by Th2 cells, signals through the IL-31RA/OSMRβ receptor on sensory neurons and keratinocytes. It is a key driver of chronic itch and is elevated in atopic dermatitis and prurigo nodularis. Blocking this axis targets the itch-scratch cycle directly, which is a different route from broad type 2 blockade for itch-dominant skin disease.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. The only readout tracked here is nemolizumab's Phase 2 PK study NCT04921345, which had no formal hypothesis test. Nemolizumab's Phase 3 trials NCT03989206 (atopic dermatitis) and NCT04204616 (prurigo nodularis) have passed primary completion, but results are not yet tracked here. They will be the first real test.

Where the openings are

  • Besides skin indications, programs tracked here include systemic sclerosis, chronic pruritus of unknown origin and ulcerative colitis, with one program each. Whether the IL-31 axis contributes to fibrosis or gut inflammation is still an open question.
  • All three programs tracked here are monoclonal antibodies. None are small molecules, bispecifics or long-acting formats. A drug that blocks both IL-31 and IL-4Rα/IL-13 might address both itch and inflammation endpoints.
  • It is untested whether itch-dominant phenotypes or serum IL-31 levels can be used to select patients. Vixarelimab targets the co-receptor OSMRβ, which also blocks OSM signaling. That gives a point of comparison with nemolizumab, which blocks IL-31RA only.
