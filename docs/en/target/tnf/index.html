Target brief

Why it matters

TNF is a central pro-inflammatory cytokine from macrophages and T cells. It drives inflammation and cell death through TNFR1, while TNFR2 supports regulatory T cells and tissue repair. Anti-TNF biologics (infliximab, adalimumab, etanercept, golimumab) have treated RA, PsA and IBD for over two decades, which gives strong clinical validation. Primary non-response, loss of response and infection risk are still unsolved.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The record tracked here is still thin. Guselkumab plus golimumab missed the MDA primary endpoint in PsA (NCT05071664), and Inmune Bio's Alzheimer's trial had no formal hypothesis test (NCT05318976). The next test is tulisokibart's Phase 3 in UC (NCT06052059), with primary completion listed for August 2026 and results not yet tracked. Tulisokibart targets TL1A, so it tests nearby TNF-superfamily biology rather than TNF itself.

Where the openings are

  • The CNS neuroinflammation hypothesis has not been properly tested: NCT05318976 was descriptive only. A trial of selective soluble-TNF inhibition could select patients by inflammatory biomarkers and be powered for a formal comparison.
  • Among programs tracked here, an oral small molecule (Phase 2) and a Sanofi bispecific (Phase 2) are rare alternatives to injectable antibodies. Whether patients who already had anti-TNF therapy, or lost response to it, benefit from these is worth testing as a pre-specified subgroup.
  • Receptor-directed approaches in oncology are early: Sichuan Clover's TNFR2-Fc fusion is in Phase 1 and a BioInvent antibody is in Phase 1/2. Whether selectively modulating TNFR1 or TNFR2 adds to immunotherapy has no readout yet.
