Target brief

Why it matters

TFPI damps the extrinsic coagulation pathway by inhibiting FXa and the TF-FVIIa complex. Blocking it increases thrombin generation and rebalances haemostasis without replacing FVIII or FIX. In principle this works in both haemophilia A and B, with or without inhibitors, and allows subcutaneous dosing.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. There is a single Phase 3 readout: NCT03938792 (marstacimab in haemophilia A, including an inhibitor cohort, n=188) met its primary endpoint. No failures or early stops are tracked here, and no trials are due to reach primary completion in the next six months, so no near-term readout will add to this record.

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, there are only two monoclonal antibodies, from Pfizer and Novo Nordisk, both in Phase 3. No siRNA, small-molecule, bispecific or long-acting engineered approaches to TFPI appear, which leaves a possible way to differentiate.
  • Rebalancing therapies have to balance efficacy against thrombotic risk. Using free-TFPI levels or thrombin-generation assays to guide dosing and select patients could help define who benefits most.
  • All tracked programs are in haemophilia A or B. Whether TFPI blockade helps in other bleeding disorders, such as von Willebrand disease, remains an untested hypothesis.

Coagulation · upstream Factor XII / Tissue factor (TF) · downstream FVIII mimetic (FIXa × FX)
Factor XII / Tissue factor (TF) → Factor XI / TFPI → FVIII mimetic (FIXa × FX) → Antithrombin
The contact path runs FXII → FXI; the extrinsic path starts with tissue factor and is held back by TFPI; both are amplified by FVIII/FIX into thrombin, which antithrombin shuts off. Haemophilia drugs rebalance via FVIII mimetics, TFPI or antithrombin.
