Target brief

Why it matters

APOC3 inhibits lipoprotein lipase and blocks the liver from clearing triglyceride-rich remnant particles. People carrying loss-of-function variants have low triglycerides and lower coronary risk, which is strong genetic validation. Severe hypertriglyceridemia can cause acute pancreatitis. APOC3 also lowers triglycerides through LPL-independent routes, so it can help in FCS, where the LPL pathway is defective.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. There are no formal readouts with NCT ids here, only positive Phase 3 topline results for plozasiran in SHASTA-3 and SHASTA-4. The next real test, for the ASO approach in sHTG, is olezarsen's NCT05681351, with primary completion due 2027-03.

Where the openings are

  • Nearly every program tracked here targets sHTG or FCS. Genetics links APOC3 to atherosclerotic risk, yet no program tracked here addresses moderate hypertriglyceridemia or cardiovascular outcomes. That gap is a hypothesis worth testing.
  • With 3 siRNA programs and 1 ASO already in play, newer entrants such as Hengrui and Zhejiang Doer need a point of difference: dosing interval, pancreatitis-event endpoints, or data in Chinese patients. NCT05681351 will give a reference point for comparing ASO with siRNA.
  • Patients could be stratified by genetics (FCS vs multifactorial chylomicronemia), baseline triglycerides and history of pancreatitis to find who benefits most. Combining with other pathways such as ANGPTL3 has not appeared among programs tracked here.
