Target brief

Why it matters

4-1BB (CD137, TNFRSF9) is a co-stimulatory receptor on activated CD8 T cells and NK cells. Through TRAF–NF-κB signaling it drives proliferation, survival and memory formation, adding an activating signal that PD-1 blockade alone does not supply. Its intracellular domain is also a standard co-stimulatory module in CAR-T constructs, so the target can be used both for agonists and in cell therapy.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. The three readouts (NCT03917381, NCT05117242, NCT06984328) all involve acasunlimab and were either untested or stopped early, because Genmab reprioritized its portfolio, not because of the mechanism. No tracked readouts are due in the next six months, so no near-term efficacy test is expected.

Where the openings are

  • With acasunlimab discontinued, the programs tracked here are Lyvgen's antibody (phase 2), invoX (phase 1) and AbelZeta's cell therapy. A testable hypothesis: conditional 4-1BB bispecifics anchored on tumor or stromal antigens other than PD-L1 could widen the therapeutic window without systemic activation.
  • Acasunlimab was tested in PD-1-experienced melanoma and metastatic NSCLC. If full data from NCT05117242 and NCT03917381 are published, they could show whether PD-L1 level or prior PD-1 exposure separates responders, which would help later programs select patients.
  • Prizloncabtagene Autoleucel is the only hematologic program tracked here (relapsed/refractory LBCL). In cell therapy, how 4-1BB co-stimulation compares with CD28 on persistence and toxicity remains an open design question.
