Target brief

Why it matters

FASN is the key enzyme of de novo lipogenesis. It builds palmitate from acetyl-CoA and malonyl-CoA. In the liver, excess lipogenesis adds to the fat buildup of MASH and is linked to inflammation and fibrosis. In sebaceous glands, FASN supplies much of the sebum lipid involved in acne. So blocking FASN has a rationale in both metabolic and skin disease.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. No readouts or failures are tracked here yet. The only tracked program is Sagimet's denifanstat in a Phase 3 acne trial, first posted in October 2026. No tracked trial reaches primary completion in the next six months, so no real test is coming soon.

Where the openings are

  • FASN is mapped to the MASH pathway, but no tracked program is in fatty liver disease. A liver-fat or fibrosis study that selects patients using MRI-PDFF or DNL markers could show whether the mechanism works in the liver.
  • Many tumors overexpress FASN and rely on it to make membrane lipids, but no oncology program is tracked here. Whether to select patients by tumor FASN expression, or combine FASN inhibition with standard therapy, is still an open question.
  • In acne, the open question is how a systemic lipogenesis inhibitor compares with existing retinoid and hormonal treatments. No skin-restricted or topical FASN inhibitor is tracked here. One could test whether suppressing sebum locally avoids systemic lipid effects.
