Target brief

Why it matters

LPA1 is a GPCR for lysophosphatidic acid (LPA), a lipid mediator. LPA1 signalling recruits fibroblasts, increases vascular leak and promotes epithelial injury, and knockout mice are protected from bleomycin-induced lung fibrosis. That makes an oral LPA1 antagonist a rational way to slow lung-function decline in IPF and other progressive fibrosing lung diseases.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is thin. Among programs tracked here, only BMS's admilparant has data: in ALOFT-IPF, 60 mg met the primary endpoint, 120 mg slowed FVC decline without reaching significance, and discontinuations matched placebo. Full data from NCT06003426, which reached primary completion in October 2026, will be the first detailed test.

Where the openings are

  • The non-significant 120 mg arm raises an exposure-response question: has the effect plateaued, or did tolerability or variability blunt the higher dose? Full NCT06003426 data and subgroup analyses could guide dose choice and patient selection.
  • Admilparant is the only LPA1 program tracked here. It is also listed for progressive pulmonary fibrosis, with no readout yet. LPA1 biology also points to fibrosis outside the lung, such as systemic sclerosis or kidney fibrosis, which no tracked program covers.
  • Discontinuations in both dose arms were similar to placebo, so adding an LPA1 antagonist on top of nintedanib or pirfenidone is a testable hypothesis. Biomarkers of LPA pathway activity might help pick the patients most likely to benefit.
