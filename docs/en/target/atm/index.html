Target brief

Why it matters

ATM is a PIKK-family kinase that is among the first activated by DNA double-strand breaks. It phosphorylates CHK2, p53 and other substrates to trigger checkpoints and homologous-recombination repair. Blocking ATM keeps tumor cells from repairing damage caused by radiation and topoisomerase inhibitors, so it is pursued mainly as a radio- and chemosensitizer and as a partner for other DDR inhibitors.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. Only one program is tracked here: CSPC Megalith's small molecule SYH2051, in phase 1/2 for breast cancer and advanced solid tumors, with no readouts yet. No trials reach primary completion in the next six months, so it is unclear when the mechanism will get its first real test.

Where the openings are

  • The tracked trials are in solid tumors with no stated combination partner. A hypothesis to test is pairing ATM inhibition with radiotherapy, topoisomerase-I chemotherapy, or ADCs carrying topo-I payloads, to see whether the sensitizing effect produces clinical benefit.
  • Patients could be selected by biomarker, such as defects in other DDR genes or abnormal p53 function, to test synthetic-lethality hypotheses. Combinations with PARP or ATR inhibitors would need close watching for added bone-marrow toxicity.
  • No brain-penetrant ATM inhibitor is tracked here. Combining one with radiation for glioma or brain metastases is an open question that could be tested. Other modalities, such as degraders, have not appeared yet.
