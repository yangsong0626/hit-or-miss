Target brief

Why it matters

KIF18A is a kinesin-8 motor that controls chromosome alignment and spindle tension in metaphase. Chromosomally unstable (CIN) tumor cells, especially those with whole-genome doubling or TP53 loss, depend on KIF18A to finish mitosis, while normal diploid cells largely do not. That gives a CIN-linked synthetic-lethal window, with high-grade serous ovarian and triple-negative breast cancer as archetypes.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. Both programs tracked here (Volastra's unnamed molecule and Accent's ATX-295) are small molecules in phase 1/2. There are no readouts, failures or early stops, and no primary completions are due in the next six months, so the mechanism is clinically untested.

Where the openings are

  • Patient selection is the key open question. It is untested whether CIN scores, whole-genome doubling, TP53 mutation or CCNE1 amplification predict response, and early expansion cohorts could settle it.
  • Programs tracked here focus on ovarian, fallopian tube and breast cancer. Other high-CIN tumors, such as squamous NSCLC or uterine serous carcinoma, look unaddressed and could be tested as indication hypotheses.
  • Only small molecules are tracked. Combinations with PARP inhibitors, platinum or taxanes, and other modalities such as degraders, remain unexplored.
