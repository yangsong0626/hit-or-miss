Target brief

Why it matters

CCR8 is expressed mainly on activated, highly suppressive tumor-infiltrating regulatory T cells (Tregs), with little expression on peripheral Tregs or effector T cells. Fc-mediated anti-CCR8 antibodies aim to clear Tregs inside the tumor and lift local immune suppression while sparing tolerance elsewhere in the body, avoiding the autoimmunity risk of CTLA-4- or CD25-directed approaches. The evidence so far is mostly preclinical.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. None of the 11 programs tracked here has a readout, failure or early stop yet. Almost all are monoclonal antibodies, mostly in phase 1/2, and LM-108 is furthest along in phase 3. The first real test is LM-108's NCT05518045, with primary completion scheduled for October 2026.

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, nearly all are monoclonal antibodies. No bispecifics (such as CCR8 x PD-1), ADCs or small molecules appear. Researchers could test whether other formats clear tumor Tregs more completely or combine more easily with other therapies.
  • Trials are concentrated in basket studies of advanced solid tumors. Only a few single-tumor trials exist: gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma, NSCLC, cervical and pancreatic cancer. Selecting patients by the density of CCR8+ Tregs in the tumor could help find who benefits.
  • BMS is running both BMS-986340 and an NF anti-CCR8 mAb, which suggests Fc design is not yet settled. Upcoming data can answer two questions: does Fc-enhanced depletion change efficacy, and does sparing peripheral Tregs lower immune-related side effects?
