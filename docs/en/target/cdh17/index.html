Target brief

Why it matters

CDH17 (liver-intestine cadherin) is an adhesion protein of the intestinal lining. It stays highly expressed in most colorectal cancers and in many gastric cancers and gastrointestinal neuroendocrine tumours, which makes it a candidate surface target for ADCs, CAR-T and similar therapies. The main worry is damage to normal gut. In healthy tissue it sits mostly at junctions between cells, where drugs may have trouble reaching it.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin: four early programs and no registry readouts. The only signal is ArriVent's initial Phase 1 data for the ADC ARR-217 in GI cancers (press release, ESMO 2026). No tracked trial reaches primary completion in the next six months.

Where the openings are

  • Among programs tracked here, three address colorectal cancer, while GI neuroendocrine tumours and gastric cancer have one each. CDH17-positive NETs and other CDH17-expressing GI tumours, such as pancreatic cancer, could be tested as less crowded settings.
  • Tracked programs are antibody-based (including the ADC ARR-217) or CAR-T (CHM-2101). None is a T-cell engager or a combination regimen. A bispecific, or an ADC paired with standard chemotherapy or immunotherapy, is an open hypothesis if the gut tolerates it.
  • The key open question is whether normal intestinal lining is spared. Full dose-escalation data from ARR-217 and CHM-2101 that compare GI toxicity and response with CDH17 expression levels would show whether selecting patients by expression widens the therapeutic window.
