Target brief

Why it matters

mTOR is a serine/threonine kinase downstream of PI3K–AKT. In two complexes, mTORC1 and mTORC2, it combines growth-factor and nutrient signals to control protein synthesis and cell growth. Loss of TSC1/TSC2 keeps mTORC1 switched on and causes tuberous sclerosis complex. Rapalogs such as everolimus have long been approved in HR+ breast cancer, renal cancer and TSC, so the mechanism is clinically validated.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record tracked here is still thin. The only readout is Aadi's tumor-agnostic phase 2 trial NCT05103358, with ORR as the primary endpoint; it was descriptive, with no formal hypothesis test. The next real test is the phase 3 everolimus trial in TSC, NCT02962414, due to reach primary completion in February 2027.

Where the openings are

  • The newer programs tracked here (AV078, sapanisertib, QLC1401, AL58805) are still in phase 1/2 to phase 2. One testable hypothesis: does selective mTORC1 inhibition reduce the metabolic toxicity rapalogs cause through mTORC2-linked pathways while keeping efficacy?
  • Only 2 neuro/psychiatry trials are tracked. Neurological diseases driven by mTORC1 overactivity, such as TSC-related epilepsy, may suit new molecules that get into the brain better.
  • Breast cancer has the most programs. Choosing patients by TSC1/TSC2 or PI3K-pathway alterations, and trying new delivery approaches such as nanoformulations (SAPU NANO), could set new programs apart from everolimus.
