Target brief

Why it matters

LAG-3 (CD223) is an inhibitory receptor on exhausted T cells. It binds MHC class II and FGL1 and is often co-expressed with PD-1. Preclinical work shows that blocking both pathways restores T-cell function synergistically. The approval of relatlimab plus nivolumab in advanced melanoma validated the target; the open question is whether the benefit carries beyond melanoma.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • Beating a strong PD-1 comparator is hard: in Phase 3, fianlimab plus cemiplimab missed statistical significance on PFS versus pembrolizumab, ahead only numerically. The only formal hit tracked here is favezelimab in Hodgkin lymphoma (NCT05508867), a disease already highly PD-1-sensitive.
  • Expansion beyond melanoma lacks controlled evidence: readouts in HCC (NCT05337137), NSCLC (NCT03516981) and early melanoma studies (NCT04303169, NCT04370704) are all safety or ORR readouts with no hypothesis test.
  • Early stops are mostly commercial: MSD ended favezelimab studies in NMIBC (NCT02625961) and Hodgkin (NCT03598608), and Roche closed tobemstomig plus tiragolumab in HCC (NCT05908786). None were stopped for safety, so these stops are not evidence against the mechanism.
  • Every Phase 3 asset tracked here is a monoclonal antibody given with the same company's PD-1 antibody. PD-1×LAG-3 bispecifics (tobemstomig, SSGJ-709, Akeso) are still in Phase 1-2.

Where the openings are

  • Two fianlimab Phase 3 melanoma trials (NCT05608291, NCT06246916) will test whether dose and partner choice can set it apart from existing LAG-3/PD-1 combinations. Biomarkers such as LAG-3 or MHC II expression could be studied alongside to find the patients who benefit.
  • Whether a bispecific extends benefit beyond melanoma depends on the tobemstomig readouts in RCC (NCT05805501) and breast cancer (NCT05852691). The favezelimab RCC studies (NCT04626518, NCT04626479) offer a comparison point.
  • The Hodgkin hit suggests that MHC II-high lymphomas may be an open indication, with few programs tracked here. The neoadjuvant setting is also worth watching; the fianlimab resectable NSCLC study (NCT06161441) is the first test.
