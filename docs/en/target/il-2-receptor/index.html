Target brief

Why it matters

IL-2 expands effector T cells and NK cells through IL-2Rβγ, but it binds high-affinity CD25 on Tregs first. High-dose aldesleukin, approved long ago for metastatic melanoma and renal cancer, validated the mechanism, but toxicity and Treg expansion limited its use. That is why newer programs mostly use engineered or targeted IL-2.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • The clinical record is still thin. Of the two readouts tracked, NCT01029873 (ALT-801 + cisplatin) had no formal statistical test and NCT05194735 (TCR-T + aldesleukin) stopped over funding. Neither tests the mechanism. No trial reaches primary completion in the next six months. Innovent's IBI363 phase 3 will be the first controlled test.

Where the openings are

  • Among trials tracked here, 25 are in oncology and only 3 in immunology/inflammation. Treg-biased low-dose IL-2 or CD25-selective agonists for autoimmune disease look like under-explored ground.
  • Cell therapies from Iovance and Alaunos still pair with systemic IL-2, while SYNCAR-001 tests orthogonal IL-2 signaling. NCT05194735 stopped for business reasons, so it is still unknown whether local or orthogonal IL-2 can replace systemic dosing with less toxicity.
  • Antibody programs such as IBI363 are in late stages but have no controlled data yet. Selecting patients by biomarkers such as PD-1-positive T-cell infiltration could set a program apart from the leaders. The phase 3 results will test this.
