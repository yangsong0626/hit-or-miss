Target brief

Why it matters

CGRP is released by trigeminal sensory neurons and drives vasodilation, neurogenic inflammation and pain sensitization. Levels rise during migraine attacks, and CGRP infusion triggers attacks in people with migraine, which validates the pathway clinically. Antibodies (erenumab, galcanezumab, fremanezumab) and gepants (rimegepant, ubrogepant) have been approved for years.

What the clinic has taught so far

  • Small-molecule gepants succeeded in both prevention and acute treatment: rimegepant (NCT05810038) and atogepant (NCT05861427) reduced monthly migraine days, and intranasal zavegepant met its acute pain-freedom endpoint (NCT05989048).
  • Late-stage trials now compete on tolerability against older drugs. Atogepant met its primary endpoint against topiramate, which was discontinuation due to adverse events (NCT05748483). Studies of fremanezumab (NCT04530110) and atogepant plus Botox (NCT05216263) produced descriptive safety data only.
  • A validated mechanism does not guarantee every trial succeeds. Erenumab's OASIS(CM) chronic migraine trial missed its primary endpoint (NCT03832998), the only efficacy failure recorded here.
  • All 9 programs tracked here are in Phase 3. All are expansions of marketed drugs into new indications or populations, and no early-stage newcomers are tracked.

Where the openings are

  • Special populations are still open. Rimegepant in menstrual migraine (NCT06641466) and eptinezumab in pediatric chronic migraine (NCT04965675) will test whether the benefit of blocking CGRP extends to these groups.
  • PACAP/PAC1 is listed as a related target, but no programs are tracked here. Patients who respond poorly to CGRP blockade, and biomarkers that predict response, may be worth exploring.
  • Combinations have only safety data so far. No program tracked here tests the efficacy of a gepant plus an antibody, or a gepant plus Botox, or covers headache types other than migraine.
