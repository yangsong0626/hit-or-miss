Hit or Miss / Trials / RISE

Averitas · Phase 3 · Neuro / psych / pain

RISE NCT04967664

Comparison of Qutenza (8% Capsaicin) With a Low-dose Capsaicin for Treatment of Nerve Pain After Surgery

Met

  • Primary endpoint “Change From Baseline in the 24-Hour Average Pain Intensity (”: LS mean difference -0.45 (95% CI -0.75–-0.14), p=0.002
  • Primary endpoint “Change From Baseline in the 24-Hour API to Week 12 [Core Pha”: LS mean difference -0.32 (95% CI -0.66–0.03), p=0.035
  • Design: randomised, quadruple-blind, active-controlled
  • actual enrollment n=412
  • Serious adverse events: Qutenza 7%; Control 4%

Likely reasons

  • The confidence interval is well away from the line of no effect; the effect is robust
  • The confidence interval is close to the line of no effect; the effect is modest, so check whether secondary endpoints and subgroups agree
  • Won against an active comparator, which is more clinically meaningful

Editor’s analysis

Met. The 8% capsaicin patch Qutenza for post-surgical neuropathic pain reduced pain scores only about 0.3–0.5 points more than a low-dose capsaicin control (0–10 scale), and the week-12 confidence interval already crossed 0. Statistically significant but of limited clinical relevance.
