Bristol Myers Squibb · Phase 3 · Oncology

INDEPENDENCE NCT04717414

An Efficacy and Safety Study of Luspatercept (ACE-536) Versus Placebo in Subjects With Myeloproliferative Neoplasm-Associated Myelofibrosis on Concomitant JAK2 Inhibitor Therapy and Who Require Red Blood Cell Transfusions

Missed

  • Primary endpoint “Red Blood Cell-transfusion Independence (RBC-TI) ≥ 12 Weeks ”: Difference in Response Rate 8.27 (95% CI -0.04–16.57), p=0.0674
  • Design: randomised, triple-blind, placebo-controlled
  • actual enrollment n=313
  • Serious adverse events: Treatment 44%; Control 38%; Cross-over 48%

Likely reasons

  • The point estimate leans one way but the confidence interval crosses the line of no effect; the effect may be small or the sample too small to detect it
  • Serious adverse event rates differ by 10 percentage points across arms; safety needs attention

Editor’s analysis

Missed. In INDEPENDENCE, in transfusion-dependent anaemic myelofibrosis patients, luspatercept raised transfusion independence only 8 points over placebo, p=0.067, favourable but not significant. Anaemia in myelofibrosis is more complex than in MDS, diluting the effect of an erythroid maturation agent, and the competitor momelotinib is already approved in this population.
