AstraZeneca · Phase 3 · Immunology / inflammation

SUNRISE NCT05398263

Tezepelumab Efficacy and Safety in Reducing Oral Corticosteroid Use in Adults With Oral Corticosteroid Dependent Asthma

Met

  • Primary endpoint “Proportion of Subjects by Categorised Percent Reduction From”: Cumulative Odds Ratio 2.93 (95% CI 1.43–6.03), p=0.003
  • Design: randomised, triple-blind, placebo-controlled
  • actual enrollment n=125
  • Serious adverse events: Tezepelumab 8%; Placebo 13%

Likely reasons

  • The confidence interval is close to the line of no effect; the effect is modest, so check whether secondary endpoints and subgroups agree
  • Placebo-controlled: it shows efficacy, but head-to-head data are needed to know whether it beats existing therapy

Editor’s analysis

Enrollment difficulty driven by changing clinical practice. SUNRISE tested tezepelumab to help patients with severe asthma reduce oral steroids, but as biologics spread, fewer patients depend long-term on oral steroids and the trial could not recruit. It actually reflects the success of biologics.

Why it stopped

Enrollment / operations · “The study was discontinued due to slow recruitment, resulting from evolving clinical practices that reduced the use of maintenance oral corticosteroids for severe asthma treatment. The termination decision was not related to safety concerns.”
