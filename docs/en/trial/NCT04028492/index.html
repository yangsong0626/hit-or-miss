Evaluating the Safety and Efficacy of Tradipitant vs. Placebo in Idiopathic and Diabetic Gastroparesis

Missed

  • Primary endpoint “Change From Baseline to Week 12 in the Biweekly Average of D”: p=0.7411
  • Design: randomised, quadruple-blind, placebo-controlled
  • actual enrollment n=979
  • Serious adverse events: Tradipitant 3%; Placebo 2%; Open Label Tradipitant 4%

Likely reasons

  • The arms are very close; the placebo response may be high

Editor’s analysis

Missed. The NK1 receptor antagonist tradipitant did not improve nausea scores over placebo in gastroparesis (p=0.74). Gastroparesis trials always have high placebo responses and subjective, noisy symptom scores, the common reason such drugs keep failing. No clear safety problem; the failure looks like insufficient efficacy or an insensitive endpoint.
